The Water That Stopped Breathing

Every summer the fish along this coast die. The cause and the outcome are known — reconstruct the chain between them.

The obvious story — “Fertilizer feeds the algae, the algae bloom uses up the oxygen, and the fish suffocate.” Half the coast believes it. It is wrong in one crucial link. Find the real one.

What you know

A river drains farm country into a shallow coastal basin. In the water are dissolved nutrients, sunlight, floating algae, bacteria, and — until midsummer — fish and bottom-dwellers.

  • Nutrient — nitrogen or phosphorus; the raw material that limits how much can grow.
  • Stratification — a warm surface layer sitting on cold deep water, so the two stop mixing.
  • Decomposer — bacteria that break down dead matter, using oxygen to do it.
  • Hypoxia — oxygen so low (under ~2 mg/L) that animals can’t survive.

Two relationships to lean on

The bacteria multiply on material that the dying algae supply to them.
The oxygen crash sets in only once the decomposers are at work.

    Design notes — what changed, and why
    Anchored endsThe trigger and the outcome are locked as “given.” You reconstruct the mechanism between a known cause and effect — less intimidating, and closer to real scientific reasoning than sorting from scratch. (This is the game’s Tutorial mode.)
    Feedback teaches both waysCheck names each link as a causal sentence — green when it holds (“decomposers feed only after the algae die”), red when it’s broken (“breaking down algae that haven’t died yet”). You learn each mechanism the moment it clicks — never a slot number, so it stays learnable but not brute-forceable.
    Misconception trapThe “obvious story” is a real, documented student error (living algae eat the oxygen). Ordering it that way breaks a link, so common-sense story-shape gives the wrong answer — Casebook’s traps-vs-truth spine, ported to sequencing.
    Reasoning gate + transferA correct order earns Silver; Gold needs you to name why the oxygen falls. The win screen shows the process’s shape and a real-world anchor (the Gulf of Mexico dead zone) so it sticks.
    One correct chainThe cascade is a single strict sequence — one right answer and a clean “you solved it,” with no ambiguity about which order counts.
