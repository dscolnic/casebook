## Mission 1 — Morning Rush

**Objective:** How doctors use clues and decide who needs help first

### M1.1 — Who Needs You First?

**Format:** TRIAGE · **Area:** TRI · **Place:** Ava, Ben, and Maya  •  Emergency Department

**Scene shown to the player**

> Three patients arrive at almost the same time. Ava sits very still and breathes hard. Ben can walk, but his ankle hurts. Maya has an itchy patch on her arm. One exam room is free. Hospitals do not always help people in the order they arrive. Some problems stay the same for an hour. Others get worse fast. Picking who goes first is called triage.

**Question**  Which patient should move to the front of the line?

**Choices offered**

- Ava, because breathing trouble can affect the whole body quickly.
- Ben, because you can see his injury and Ava is sitting quietly.
- Maya, because a rash might spread to other people in the room.
- Whoever arrived first, so nobody waits longer than anybody else.

**Correct answer**

**Ava, because breathing trouble can affect the whole body quickly.**

**Why (shown in verdict):** Breathing brings oxygen into the body. If breathing is difficult, the team needs to check that patient quickly.

**Why the others do not hold**

- B. Ben, because ankle pain always comes before breathing problems. — Pain matters, but Ben is able to walk and breathe normally.
- C. Maya, because itchy skin is always the most dangerous clue. — An itchy patch matters, but Maya is breathing and talking comfortably.
- D. See them in alphabetical order so the choice is fair. — Triage is not about alphabetical order; it is about which problem could become dangerous fastest.

**Takeaway:** Doctors use urgency, not first-come-first-served order, when several patients need help at once.

### M1.2 — Observation or Measurement?

**Format:** CHOICE · **Area:** TRI · **Place:** Luis  •  Exam Room 3

**Scene shown to the player**

> Luis says his belly hurts. You can see that he is awake and talking. A nurse checks his temperature with a thermometer. She counts his pulse too. All of it goes on his chart. But the clues are not all the same kind. Some you notice. Some the patient tells you. Some come from a tool, as a number.

**Question**  Which item is a measurement?

**Choices offered**

- Luis says his belly hurts.
- Luis looks worried.
- His temperature is 98.7°F.
- He skipped breakfast.

**Correct answer**

**His temperature is 98.7°F.**

**Why (shown in verdict):** A measurement is a number found with a tool or a careful count. A thermometer gives a temperature number.

**Why the others do not hold**

- A. Luis says his belly hurts. — Pain is important information, but it is something Luis reports, not a tool measurement.
- B. Luis looks worried. — Looking worried is an observation.
- D. He skipped breakfast. — Skipping breakfast is part of the story or history.

**Takeaway:** Doctors combine observations, patient stories, and measurements. They are different kinds of clues.

### M1.3 — Your First Exam Room

**Format:** SEQUENCE · **Area:** TRI · **Place:** Ben  •  Exam Room 5

**Scene shown to the player**

> Ben is ready for his ankle check. This is your first time running the room. Hospitals do the same steps in the same order every time. That can feel slow. It is how a busy team makes sure nothing is missed. Clean hands, Ben's story, a careful look, and a clear note all have a place. Someone else will read that note later.

**Question**  Put these steps in the best order.

**Cards to order** (presented shuffled)

- Clean your hands, so nothing from the last patient travels to Ben.
- Ask Ben what happened, and where it hurts most.
- Look at the ankle, using what Ben said to know where to look.
- Write down the clues, so the next person sees what you saw.

**Correct answer**

1. **Clean your hands, so nothing from the last patient travels to Ben.**
2. **Ask Ben what happened, and where it hurts most.**
3. **Look at the ankle, using what Ben said to know where to look.**
4. **Write down the clues, so the next person sees what you saw.**

**Why (shown in verdict):** Each step makes the next one work. Hands first, because whatever is on them goes onto Ben. His story before the exam, because it tells you where to look — an exam with no story is a search. Writing last, because it records the other three.

**Takeaway:** Each step in a hospital routine is there to make the next one work.

## Mission 2 — Breathing Room

**Objective:** Lungs, air, oxygen, and breathing rate

### M2.1 — Follow One Breath

**Format:** SEQUENCE · **Area:** RESP · **Place:** Priya  •  Respiratory Room

**Scene shown to the player**

> Priya takes a deep breath. She asks where the air goes. The therapist hands you four picture cards. Every cell in her body needs oxygen. No cell can go and get it by itself. So the breathing system brings air deep inside. There the blood picks the oxygen up. Knowing the path helps you find where a problem is.

**Question**  Put the path of a breath in order.

**Cards to order** (presented shuffled)

- Air enters through the nose or mouth.
- Air moves down the windpipe.
- Air reaches the lungs.
- Oxygen moves into the blood to travel around the body.

**Correct answer**

1. **Air enters through the nose or mouth.**
2. **Air moves down the windpipe.**
3. **Air reaches the lungs.**
4. **Oxygen moves into the blood to travel around the body.**

**Why (shown in verdict):** Air must travel through the breathing tubes to the lungs before oxygen can move into the blood.

**Takeaway:** The lungs are the meeting place between outside air and the blood.

### M2.2 — The Dusty-Room Clue

**Format:** CHOICE · **Area:** RESP · **Place:** Noah  •  Pediatric Wing

**Scene shown to the player**

> Noah helped clean a dusty storage room. Now he is wheezing. He can still talk. Each breath makes a small whistling sound. The team is with him. One clue by itself does not mean much. The useful question is which body part could make that clue. A whistle needs air squeezing through a narrow space. So the clue points somewhere.

**Question**  Which body system should the team focus on first?

**Choices offered**

- The breathing system — the airways and lungs.
- The throat and voice box, because that is where whistling sounds are made.
- The heart and blood vessels, because a whistle could be blood rushing.
- The digestive system, because Noah breathed in dust and swallowed some.

**Correct answer**

**The breathing system — the airways and lungs.**

**Why (shown in verdict):** Wheezing is a sound made when air moves through narrowed breathing tubes.

**Why the others do not hold**

- B. The digestive system — the stomach and intestines. — Stomach problems do not usually make a whistling sound with each breath.
- C. The skeleton — the bones and joints. — Bones support the body but do not move air in and out.
- D. The urinary system — the kidneys and bladder. — Kidneys help balance water and clean the blood; they do not make breathing sounds.

**Takeaway:** A clue is useful when you connect it to the body system that could produce it.

### M2.3 — Count the Breaths

**Format:** BALLPARK · **Area:** RESP · **Place:** Mrs. Grant  •  Nurse Station

**Scene shown to the player**

> Mrs. Grant is resting. The nurse counts 5 breaths in 15 seconds. She asks you for the one minute number. Nurses count in short blocks because it is faster. People also breathe differently when they know you are watching. Fifteen seconds is one quarter of a minute. This is math practice only. The care team decides what numbers mean.

**Question**  5 breaths in 15 seconds is about how many breaths in 60 seconds?

**Givens**

- Use the numbers shown

**Choices offered**

- 10
- 15
- 20
- 60

**Correct answer**

Equation shown: `{0} × {1}`
Tiles offered: `5 breaths  (counted)`, `4  (fifteen-second parts in a minute)`, `15 seconds  (how long she counted)`, `60 seconds  (one minute)`, `2  (halves of a minute)`
Tiles that belong: `5 breaths  (counted)`, `4  (fifteen-second parts in a minute)`
Decoy tiles: `15 seconds  (how long she counted)`, `60 seconds  (one minute)`, `2  (halves of a minute)`
Formula: `a*b`
**Target: 20 breaths per minute** (tolerance ±0.5)
Explanation shown: A minute holds four fifteen-second parts, so a fifteen-second count is multiplied by four. Counting for the short window and scaling up is how it is done at the bedside — a full minute of standing still changes how somebody breathes.
Book's worked answer: 20

**Why (shown in verdict):** One minute has four 15-second parts. 5 × 4 = 20.

**Why the others do not hold**

- A. 10 — 10 would be only two groups of 5.
- B. 15 — 15 would be three groups of 5.
- D. 60 — 60 is the number of seconds in a minute, not the estimated number of breaths.

**Takeaway:** Doctors often turn short counts into one-minute estimates.

## Mission 3 — Heart on the Move

**Objective:** The heart, blood, pulse, and circulation

### M3.1 — The Blood Loop

**Format:** SEQUENCE · **Area:** RESP · **Place:** Mr. Cho  •  Cardiology Corner

**Scene shown to the player**

> A heart model sits on the table. Four arrow cards lie beside it. The nurse says blood does not travel one way and stop. It goes around and around, all day and all night. It picks up oxygen, comes back, drops it off, and returns. The heart is the pump that keeps it moving. Put the arrows in order.

**Question**  Put the simplified blood loop in order, starting at the heart.

**Cards to order** (presented shuffled)

- The heart pumps blood toward the lungs.
- In the lungs, blood picks up oxygen.
- The heart pumps oxygen-rich blood to the body.
- Blood returns from the body to the heart.

**Correct answer**

1. **The heart pumps blood toward the lungs.**
2. **In the lungs, blood picks up oxygen.**
3. **The heart pumps oxygen-rich blood to the body.**
4. **Blood returns from the body to the heart.**

**Why (shown in verdict):** The heart keeps blood moving so it can pick up oxygen and deliver it around the body.

**Takeaway:** Circulation is a loop, not a one-way trip.

### M3.2 — Pulse Math

**Format:** BALLPARK · **Area:** RESP · **Place:** Jordan  •  Rehab Hall

**Scene shown to the player**

> Jordan just climbed the stairs. Now he is resting. The therapist counts 18 beats in 15 seconds. Each heartbeat pushes blood along. That push makes a little bump you can feel. Counting it needs only fingers and a clock. It is one of the oldest checks in medicine. Fifteen seconds is a quarter of a minute.

**Question**  18 beats in 15 seconds is about how many beats in 60 seconds?

**Givens**

- Use the numbers shown

**Choices offered**

- 36
- 54
- 72
- 180

**Correct answer**

Equation shown: `{0} × {1}`
Tiles offered: `18 beats  (counted)`, `4  (fifteen-second parts in a minute)`, `15 seconds  (how long she counted)`, `60 seconds  (one minute)`, `3  (twenty-second parts in a minute)`
Tiles that belong: `18 beats  (counted)`, `4  (fifteen-second parts in a minute)`
Decoy tiles: `15 seconds  (how long she counted)`, `60 seconds  (one minute)`, `3  (twenty-second parts in a minute)`
Formula: `a*b`
**Target: 72 beats per minute** (tolerance ±0.5)
Explanation shown: The same scaling as a breathing count, and the same reason for the short window. Note what it is not: a number that decides anything on its own. What the team watches is the same person’s count before and after, and whether it settles.
Book's worked answer: 72

**Why (shown in verdict):** There are four 15-second parts in a minute. 18 × 4 = 72.

**Why the others do not hold**

- A. 36 — 36 is only 18 × 2.
- B. 54 — 54 is 18 × 3.
- D. 180 — 180 is 18 × 10.

**Takeaway:** A pulse is a clue that the heart is beating and moving blood.

### M3.3 — Why the Faster Pulse?

**Format:** CHOICE · **Area:** RESP · **Place:** Jordan  •  Treatment Room

**Scene shown to the player**

> Jordan's pulse was slow while he sat. It was faster right after the stairs. He feels fine now. The doctor asks you why it changed. A pulse is not one fixed number for a person. It goes up and down during the day. Body parts also work as a team. When one changes, another often changes too.

**Question**  What is the best explanation?

**Choices offered**

- Working muscles need more oxygen, so the heart moves blood faster.
- The heart beats faster to help the body cool down after exercise.
- Climbing stairs shakes the body, which makes the heartbeat easier to feel.
- The pulse only seems faster because Jordan is paying attention to it.

**Correct answer**

**Working muscles need more oxygen, so the heart moves blood faster.**

**Why (shown in verdict):** Active muscles use more oxygen and fuel. Faster circulation helps deliver what they need.

**Why the others do not hold**

- B. The bones need to become heavier during exercise. — Bones do not become heavier each time we exercise.
- C. The stomach stops existing while a person runs. — The stomach is still there and working even while we move.
- D. The skin tells the heart to stop pumping. — The skin helps with temperature and protection, but it does not tell the heart to stop.

**Takeaway:** Body systems work together. Muscles, lungs, blood, and heart all change during exercise.

### M4.2 — Where Are the Nutrients Going?

### M4.3 — Three Jobs, Three Organs

## Mission 5 — Water Watch

**Objective:** Hydration, kidneys, urine, and water balance

### M5.1 — The Thirsty Player

**Format:** CHOICE · **Area:** NUTR · **Place:** Mateo  •  Sports Medicine Bay

**Scene shown to the player**

> Mateo just finished a long soccer practice. He is thirsty. His mouth feels dry. The nurse says his urine looks darker than usual. The team is checking him. Water is in the blood, in every cell, and in sweat. The body loses some when you are active. It also gives signals when water runs low. Doctors read several signals together.

**Question**  What body need do these clues most strongly point to?

**Choices offered**

- The body may need more water.
- The body may need more food, because practice used up his energy.
- The body may need more sleep after a long practice.
- The body may be too warm and need to cool down.

**Correct answer**

**The body may need more water.**

**Why (shown in verdict):** Thirst and dry mouth are common ways the body signals that it needs water.

**Why the others do not hold**

- B. The body needs a new bone. — Nothing in the clues points to a bone injury.
- C. The lungs have stopped moving air. — Mateo is talking and breathing comfortably.
- D. The ears need louder sounds. — The clues are not about hearing.

**Takeaway:** Water is needed throughout the body, and the body has signals that help protect its water balance.

### M5.2 — The Drink Tracker

**Format:** BALLPARK · **Area:** NUTR · **Place:** Mrs. Lee  •  Nurse Station

**Scene shown to the player**

> Mrs. Lee has a drink tracker by her bed. It shows 2 cups in the morning, 1 cup with lunch and 3 cups later. On the wall is a chart saying a body her size loses about 8 cups of water a day — through breathing, through sweat, and into urine. That loss happens whether or not anybody is drinking. This is a maths problem, not drinking advice.

**Question**  How many cups short of what her body lost is she?

**Givens**

- Use the numbers shown

**Choices offered**

- 4
- 5
- 6
- 7

**Correct answer**

Equation shown: `{3} − ( {0} + {1} + {2} )`
Tiles offered: `2 cups  (morning)`, `1 cup  (with lunch)`, `3 cups  (later)`, `8 cups  (what her body loses in a day)`, `10 cups  (what the jug holds)`, `3 rows  (how many entries)`
Tiles that belong: `2 cups  (morning)`, `1 cup  (with lunch)`, `3 cups  (later)`, `8 cups  (what her body loses in a day)`
Decoy tiles: `10 cups  (what the jug holds)`, `3 rows  (how many entries)`
Formula: `d - (a + b + c)`
**Target: 2 cups** (tolerance ±0.5)
Explanation shown: Water going in has to keep up with water going out, and the tracker only shows one side of that. The jug is how much she could drink, not how much she did.
Book's worked answer: 6

**Why (shown in verdict):** Six cups in against about eight cups out leaves her two short. The body loses water all day without being asked, which is why the amount that goes in has to be counted against it rather than on its own.

**Why the others do not hold**

- A. 4 — 4 leaves out two of the cups.
- B. 5 — 5 is one cup too few.
- D. 7 — 7 is one cup too many.

**Takeaway:** Water balance is what goes in measured against what goes out, not either one alone.

## Mission 6 — X-Ray Day

**Objective:** Bones, joints, protection, and medical imaging

### M6.1 — Why Take an X-Ray?

**Format:** CHOICE · **Area:** MOVE · **Place:** Eli  •  Emergency Imaging

**Scene shown to the player**

> Eli fell off his bike. His wrist hurts and looks swollen. The doctor needs to know if a bone is broken. The bone is under the skin. Nobody can see it from outside. Hospitals have many tools. Each one answers a different question. Picking the right tool matters. The wrong tool answers a question nobody asked.

**Question**  Which hospital tool best matches that job?

**Choices offered**

- An X-ray image
- A thermometer
- A stethoscope
- A tape measure, to record how swollen the wrist is

**Correct answer**

**An X-ray image**

**Why (shown in verdict):** X-rays can make pictures that show bones well, helping clinicians check for breaks.

**Why the others do not hold**

- B. A hearing test — A hearing test checks how well someone hears.
- C. A food scale — A food scale measures mass.
- D. An eye chart — An eye chart checks vision.

**Takeaway:** Doctors choose tools based on the kind of clue they need.

### M6.2 — Skeleton Jobs

**Format:** CHOICE · **Area:** MOVE · **Place:** Rosa, Mr. Bell, and Tess  •  Orthopedic Room

**Scene shown to the player**

> The bone doctor has three cards to hand out. Rosa needs the part that protects the heart and lungs. Mr. Bell needs the part that holds up the back. Tess needs the place where bones meet and move. Bones do more than hold you up. They are not all the same shape. That is because they do not all do the same job.

**Question**  Which set is correct?

**Choices offered**

- Rosa—rib cage; Mr. Bell—spine; Tess—joint
- Rosa—spine; Mr. Bell—rib cage; Tess—joint
- Rosa—rib cage; Mr. Bell—joint; Tess—spine
- Rosa—skull; Mr. Bell—spine; Tess—joint

**Correct answer**

**Rosa—rib cage; Mr. Bell—spine; Tess—joint**

**Why (shown in verdict):** The rib cage protects the chest organs, the spine supports the back and protects the spinal cord, and joints are places where bones meet.

**Why the others do not hold**

- B. Rosa—stomach; Mr. Bell—lungs; Tess—kidney — Those body parts do not match the skeletal jobs described.
- C. Rosa—skin; Mr. Bell—heart; Tess—bladder — The heart is protected by the rib cage, not used as a back support.
- D. Rosa—brain; Mr. Bell—intestine; Tess—blood — Blood and intestines are not joints or bones.

**Takeaway:** The skeleton supports, protects, and helps the body move.

### M6.3 — Possible Broken Bone

**Format:** SEQUENCE · **Area:** MOVE · **Place:** Eli  •  Cast and Brace Hall

**Scene shown to the player**

> Eli's wrist may be hurt. He looks worried. You are the junior doctor in this game. You do not fix the wrist. Trained adults do that. Your job is to keep him still and calm. Moving a hurt arm can make it worse. Getting the team what they need fast is a real medical job. It is the one that is yours.

**Question**  Put the safe hospital response in order.

**Cards to order** (presented shuffled)

- Stop Eli using the wrist, because moving a broken bone can damage what is around it.
- Tell the nurse or doctor, so trained help is already coming.
- Let the trained team examine the wrist and decide about an image.
- Record what happened and what the team found.

**Correct answer**

1. **Stop Eli using the wrist, because moving a broken bone can damage what is around it.**
2. **Tell the nurse or doctor, so trained help is already coming.**
3. **Let the trained team examine the wrist and decide about an image.**
4. **Record what happened and what the team found.**

**Why (shown in verdict):** Keeping the wrist still comes first because it is the only step that prevents more damage; every later step can happen while it is being kept still. Telling an adult is next because help takes time to arrive, and the record comes last because it records everything before it.

**Takeaway:** The first action is the one that stops things getting worse; the rest can happen while it holds.

## Mission 7 — Rehab Race

**Objective:** Muscles, joints, movement, and practice

### M7.1 — Make the Hand Move

**Format:** SEQUENCE · **Area:** MOVE · **Place:** Sophie  •  Muscle Lab Corner

**Scene shown to the player**

> Sophie wiggles her fingers. She asks how the message got there so fast. The therapist gives you four cards. Moving one finger takes a whole chain. First a decision. Then a message along a nerve. Then a muscle pulls. Then a bone moves at a joint. Therapists think about this chain a lot. When movement fails, the chain shows where to look.

**Question**  Put the movement pathway in order.

**Cards to order** (presented shuffled)

- The brain decides to move the hand.
- Nerves carry the message toward the muscles.
- Muscles tighten and pull.
- Bones move at the joints.

**Correct answer**

1. **The brain decides to move the hand.**
2. **Nerves carry the message toward the muscles.**
3. **Muscles tighten and pull.**
4. **Bones move at the joints.**

**Why (shown in verdict):** The nervous system sends the message, and the muscles create the pulling force that moves bones.

**Takeaway:** Movement is teamwork between the brain, nerves, muscles, bones, and joints.

### M7.2 — Tired Muscles

**Format:** CHOICE · **Area:** MOVE · **Place:** Amara  •  Physical Therapy Gym

**Scene shown to the player**

> Amara has done the same gentle motion many times. Her arm feels tired now. Nothing hurts sharply. There is no new injury. The therapist stops the set and lets her rest. Muscles use energy every time they pull. Doing one motion over and over uses it up fast. Tired is not the same as hurt. Therapists plan rest for that reason.

**Question**  Why is rest a sensible next step in this scene?

**Choices offered**

- Muscles can get tired after repeated work and need recovery time.
- The arm is injured, and rest is how an injury heals.
- Muscles only grow while a person is resting, so the set is finished.
- Tiredness means the therapy is not working and should be changed.

**Correct answer**

**Muscles can get tired after repeated work and need recovery time.**

**Why (shown in verdict):** Muscles use energy when they work. After repeated activity, a planned rest can be part of therapy.

**Why the others do not hold**

- B. Rest makes bones disappear. — Bones do not disappear during rest.
- C. The heart stops forever whenever muscles get tired. — The heart keeps beating all the time.
- D. The lungs only work while a person is asleep. — The lungs work whether a person is awake or asleep.

**Takeaway:** Body tissues can change their activity depending on what the body is doing.

### M7.3 — Therapy Sets

**Format:** BALLPARK · **Area:** MOVE · **Place:** Mr. Diaz  •  Rehab Track

**Scene shown to the player**

> Mr. Diaz is doing gentle leg lifts. His therapist picked 3 sets of 5. The therapist chose the plan, not you. Therapy uses sets because short rests help muscles. Counting in groups is also easier to track. A nurse can count to five while talking. Fifteen is harder. Groups of the same size are what multiplying is for.

**Question**  How many leg lifts are in 3 sets of 5?

**Givens**

- Use the numbers shown

**Choices offered**

- 8
- 10
- 15
- 35

**Correct answer**

Equation shown: `{0} × {1}`
Tiles offered: `3 sets`, `5 lifts in a set`, `2 rest breaks`, `10 minutes  (how long the session runs)`, `1 leg at a time`
Tiles that belong: `3 sets`, `5 lifts in a set`
Decoy tiles: `2 rest breaks`, `10 minutes  (how long the session runs)`, `1 leg at a time`
Formula: `a*b`
**Target: 15 leg lifts** (tolerance ±0.5)

3. **Tell the team what she was exposed to and when it started.**
4. **Write down what you saw, in the order it happened.**

**Why (shown in verdict):** Calling is first because it is the only step whose clock is running whether or not you do anything else. Watching breathing comes next because it is the fastest-changing thing about her. The exposure and its timing are what the team needs before they act, and the written record is what stops the order of events being remembered wrong.

**Takeaway:** In an emergency the first action is the one with a clock on it, and the last is the one that protects the record.

## Mission 15 — The Super Shift

**Objective:** Putting body systems, evidence, triage, and teamwork together

### M15.1 — Six Patients, One First Room

**Format:** TRIAGE · **Area:** TRI · **Place:** Nora, DeShawn, Mei, Mr. Alvarez, Fatima, and Owen  •  Central Triage

**Scene shown to the player**

**Choices offered**

- Mr. Alvarez, because feeling lightheaded could mean he is about to faint.
- Mei, because a fever could spread to the other people waiting.
- DeShawn, because he has been standing on a sore ankle the longest.

**Correct answer**

**Nora, because significant breathing trouble can affect the whole body quickly.**

**Why (shown in verdict):** The course’s most important triage pattern is that breathing trouble can become dangerous quickly and deserves immediate professional attention.

**Why the others do not hold**

- B. DeShawn, because ankle pain always comes before breathing. — A walking ankle injury can usually wait behind major breathing trouble.
- C. Fatima, because itchy eyes are always an emergency. — Itchy eyes may be uncomfortable but do not outrank significant breathing difficulty.
- D. Owen, because a covered scrape must always be seen before everyone else. — A small covered scrape does not outrank a breathing emergency.

**Takeaway:** Triage means asking, “Which problem could become dangerous fastest?”
