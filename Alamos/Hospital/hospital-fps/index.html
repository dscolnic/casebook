Children's Hospital — your first shift

It is eight in the morning and the department is already full. Fifteen shifts stand between you and the end of your rotation, and every one of them has children in it who are waiting on somebody working out what is wrong. You are the junior doctor. You do not prescribe and you do not treat — you look, you measure, you think, and you tell the team what the evidence says. The clock in the corner is the thing that makes it hard: beds are finite, the queue is not, and a child waiting for your answer is a child nobody else is looking at. Take too long and the day runs out with people still waiting. Take a shortcut and somebody gets sent to the wrong room.

Being wrong is survivable here — the team explains it, and you go again. Losing the day is not. Watch the clock.
