# Overwind — round 2

v1.2 builds, imports and plays. The delivery landed: the **Safe Winding Plan**
and its twelve parts are on the board in the winder house and named on the
opening card, and the campaign closes on your own three paragraphs. Both of
round 1's blocking items are done.

Eight findings left, and they are all card-level. In the order they matter.

---

## 1. Twelve mission cards never say what the player decides

Unchanged from round 1, and it is twelve of twelve. Every stake in this set is
written so the player can see their own job in it:

> **Today you decide** whether the proposed start can be used for passenger trips.

Yours say what is happening and stop. Your objective line already carries the
decision — *"Can the proposed start be used for passenger trips?"* — so this is
moving one clause up into the stake, not new writing.

Worth knowing: Boomtown and Whiteout both cleared this in one pass, so the shape
is proven against your own house style.

## 2. Eight segues do not turn

The segue carries one mission into the next and its job is a **complication** —
*but*, *yet*, *so*, *now*, *that leaves*. Eight of the twelve state a fact and
stop. Two more name no number, clock or person, and one (M5) repeats the next
mission's stake word for word.

## 3. Eleven equations are printed and never computed

The mission card's "Worth knowing first" box shows the day's equations. On
eleven of the twelve days, no stop that day actually computes the one shown:

```
day 1  shows  v = dy/dt; a = dv/dt; Δy = ∫v dt   and no stop that day uses it
day 2  shows  I = ∫r² dm; v = Rω                 and no stop that day uses it
```

Either the day needs a stop that computes it, or the equation should not be on
that day's card. This is the largest single finding in the campaign and it is
the one a student would notice: the card teaches a relationship the day never
asks for.

## 4. Six derivations never substitute a number

`deriveGivens` reads every DERIVE line and asks whether the arithmetic is done.
Six of your twelve are algebra all the way down — symbols rearranged, no number
ever put in — and one substitutes a number the player was never given. On a
course whose exam is computed, a derivation that never computes is half the
exercise. One numeric line each.

## 5. Two closing cards read above the ceiling

M11 at grade 7.1 and M12 at 6.6, against 6.5. Both are one long sentence. Every
other card in the campaign is under it now — mean 3.6, worst 4.9 — so these two
are the last of that work.

## 6. One concept claims a prerequisite taught later

Stop 19 (day 5) claims *"Energy and momentum in rotating systems"*, which your
§5 says is built out of *"Work and potential energy"* — and that is not claimed
until later. Either move the stop or correct the prerequisite.

## 7. `MJ` is used and never introduced

One term, in the energy missions. A gloss on first use.

## 8. Six passages, no questions

The six roster bios are readable and none carries a question, so reading them is
optional in the way that means nobody does.

## On our side, no action wanted

The place needed nothing renamed — same mine, same course, same cast — and it is
dressed since round 1: spoil heap, tub road and tubs, rope coils, fan house and
duct, settling ponds, weighbridge, and out on the moor a dry-stone wall with a
stile, sheep, peat cuttings, the survey cairns and a telegraph line. The cage
runs the frame with the sheave wheels turning, the shift board gains a slip a
mission, and the passenger gate opens on day twelve.

One reader fixed for this bible since round 1: a board whose four keys are two
lists, a mapping and a sentence — your PROTOCOL stops — was being read with the
mapping as the board and both columns thrown away, so five boards arrived with
no options at all.
