## 1. The keyed answer is the longest option in 16 of 19

```
mission questions: the keyed answer is the longest option in 16 of 19 (84%)
    length alone identifies the answer — p = 1.0e-7
```

A student who has learned nothing can score 84% by picking the longest line.
Chance is 25%; one in ten million says this is not chance. It happens naturally
— the right answer carries the qualification, the distractors are blunt — and it
is the single most damaging pattern a multiple-choice campaign can have.

The fix is not to shorten the key. It is to give the distractors the same
grammatical weight:

> ✗ **Record higher demand as the cause of the lunch price rise, since orders
>    rose at the unchanged price** / Record falling supply / Movement along demand
>
> ✓ Record higher demand, since orders rose at the unchanged price /
>   Record falling supply, since the input cost rose with the price /
>   Record movement along demand, since the price rose and quantity fell

Same length, same shape, and the difference is the economics rather than the word
count.

## 2. Twenty-nine concept-ordering problems

The largest content finding, and the measurement is against **your own §5
Prerequisites column**. Two shapes:

- **21 stops claim a concept whose prerequisite no stop ever teaches.** Concept
  3, *"Marginal choice and utility per dollar"*, is listed as a prerequisite of
  concept 4 and is claimed by no stop in the campaign. Either a stop should teach
  it or it should not be listed.
- **8 stops sit on the same day as their own prerequisite.** Day 1 teaches
  concept 1 at stop 1 and claims concept 2 at stops 2, 3 and 4. That may be
  deliberate — a day that builds on itself — but it is worth confirming.

## 3. Thirteen segues do not turn

The segue's job is a complication — *but*, *yet*, *so*, *now*, *that leaves*.
Thirteen of fifteen state a fact and stop.

## 4. Twelve stakes run 44 to 49 words

The card is read in about fifteen seconds, and the stake is one of four blocks
on it. Two sentences.

## 5. Twelve equations are printed and never computed

The "Worth knowing first" box shows the day's equations; on twelve of fifteen
days no stop that day computes the one shown — day 1 shows
`OC = forgone output / gained output`, day 2 shows `Qd = Qs`. Either the day
needs a stop that computes it, or it should not be on that day's card.

## 6. `MRP` is used and never introduced, and five passages carry no question

One term, on the factor-market missions. And the five bios are readable with
nothing to answer.
