bars, worked examples, every stop reachable and every stop asked at a fixture

Two things are missing that only you can write, and one measurement worth
reading before the rest.

---

## 1. The fifteen pieces of what the island prepares

Every campaign in this set builds **one named thing, a piece a mission** — the
opening card names it, each mission hands over that day's piece, and a board in
one room shows all fifteen. Wildtype names nothing, so the board in the Field
Planning Room is still showing **Dark Fibre's**: "The Repair Order", and its
twelve pieces are about a submarine cable.

Your §7.1 ledger already writes the fifteen, one per mission:

> | 1 | The incomplete feed is withdrawn and replacement nutrients reach the nursery. |
> | 3 | Ventilated pot lids are fitted after the sealed-pot test. |
> | 13 | Receiving plots are prepared and only the instrumented contained pilot proceeds. |

What is needed is a **name** for the whole thing — the permitted contained
pilot is the obvious candidate, since it is what the fifteen days are for — and
a **short name per piece**:

```yaml
delivery:
  name: "The Contained Pilot"          # or the permit, or the manifest
  what: "…one sentence: what it is and who reads it."
  pieces:
    - "The feed correction"            # M1
    - "The matched rinse"              # M2
    - "The ventilated lids"            # M3
    - …fifteen in all, one per mission
```

Note that it must be **fifteen**, not twelve: the count has to match the
missions or the last day hands over nothing.

## 3. Thirteen concept-ordering problems

Measured against **your own §5 Prerequisites column**: we build a syllabus from
it, and then check that a stop claiming a concept comes after a stop that taught
what it is built out of. Thirteen stops claim a concept whose stated
prerequisite **no stop in the campaign ever teaches** — concept 2,
"Macromolecules and hydrolysis", is built out of concept 1, "Water and carbon
chemistry", and concept 1 is claimed by nobody.

With thirty concepts and sixty stops, half the spine cannot be taught, so this
is expected to some degree — but a prerequisite that nothing teaches is either a
missing stop or a prerequisite that should not be listed.

## 4. Fifteen mission cards never say what the player decides

*"Today you decide whether to replace the failing seedling feed."* Fifteen of
fifteen say what is happening and never turn to the player. Your objective line
is close enough that one clause does it.

## 5. Thirteen segues do not turn

The segue's job is a complication — *but*, *yet*, *so*, *now*, *that leaves*.
Thirteen state a fact and stop; two more name no number, clock or person.

## On our side, no action wanted

The place is Dark Fibre's ground, reshaped to your §3 exactly — six rooms on the
same footprints, renamed and re-described, with all eighteen declared fixtures
built and every one of your placement lines resolved. It was four grey sheds on
sand; it is an island now: nursery beds under hoop tunnels, a marsh with reed
beds and a boardwalk out to a hide with birds circling over it, a grazing
enclosure, a seed orchard, rock pools and a strand line, hedging on the seaward
side, and the ship alongside its jetty. Two of your missions drive it — the
seedling trays go pale to green as the campaign advances, the lamp trial lights
one tunnel on M4 and stays that way because M5 halts the wider reset, the
ventilated lids appear after M3, and the sample cart gains a box a mission and
goes up the gangway on M15.

Two things the build learned from this bible:

- your fixture tables are keyed `| ID | Fixture | Build | Wall | Exact caption |`
  with the area in the heading above them, and neither the ID column nor the
  heading was being read — all thirty-six of your declared objects came back as
  none;
- **a person may own two areas.** `**Area ownership:** SEED, GENE.` matched no
  group at all, so your conservation geneticist was posted to whichever area the
  base game happened to have and both of her rooms read as having nobody in them.
