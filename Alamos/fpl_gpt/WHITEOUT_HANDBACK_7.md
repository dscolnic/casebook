# Whiteout — round 7

v2.13 builds, imports and plays, and it is the cleanest of the four. Round 6's
two items are done: **The Recovery Record** and its fifteen pieces are on the
board in the Operations Module and named on the opening card, and the campaign
closes on your own three paragraphs — the plane landing, its lights crossing the
snow, the Runway Door. Every stake says what the player decides. The cards came
down from grade 9.3 to 3.0, none over the ceiling; scenes name somebody 60 of 60.

Five findings left, all small.

---

## 1. Nine segue lines repeat something they should not

- **four** repeat that mission's own takeaway word for word. The takeaway is the
  principle and the segue is the drama; they are not the same job.
- **two** repeat the next mission's stake verbatim, so the player reads the same
  sentence twice in ninety seconds.
- **one** states a fact and stops — no *but*, *yet*, *so*, *now*.

## 2. Five primer lines run 37 to 49 words

Under "Worth knowing first", where the card is read in about fifteen seconds and
the box is one of four blocks on it. Two sentences each.

## 3. The opening and the closing card do not name anybody

```
the opening blurb: name somebody
the closing card:  name somebody
```

Elena Park is in the opening card's last sentence as a speaker, which is right —
what the check wants is a person *in the prose*, doing something, before the
quote. The ending has the same gap: it addresses the player — *"you checked the
instruments…"* — and never says who else was there. Mei Alvarez asking whether
the instruments agree because reality agrees or because they share code is the
best line in the bible and she is in neither card.

Related: the opening's last line, *"Prove each change before it goes live."*, is
a closing line with no number and no clock in it. The rest of that card is
specific — 28 people, 36 hours — and the line that lands is the general one.

## 4. Two derivations do not compute

One substitutes a number the player was never given; one never substitutes at
all. Twelve derivations, two to look at. Say the word and we will name the steps.

## 5. Nine terms are used and never introduced

`shut`, `scrubber`, `ppm`, `vent`, `lock`, `hab`, `abort`, `rms`, `go`.

Several are panel labels — GO, LOCK, VENT, ABORT — which is exactly where a word
needs a gloss the first time it appears: a button is not a definition. `rms`,
`ppm`, `scrubber` and `hab` are the four a reader could not guess.

## 6. Two equations are printed and never computed, and six passages carry no question

M1 shows `percent = 100.0 × delivered / requested` and M10 shows
`binary-search work ≈ log₂(n)`, and no stop on either day computes the one on its
card. M1's is arguable — stop 1 traces it as code, which is the right question
and not a calculation — so this may be an equation that should come off the card
rather than a stop that should be added.

## On our side, no action wanted

Two things were still Ice Core's and are not any more. The site was named
**Vestri Dome Station**, and a twenty-two metre **drill tower** stood over a
trench at the far end of the route — the previous campaign's entire silhouette,
in a campaign about software on a research station. Same lattice, same height,
same collider: it is the guyed **communications mast** your §3 asks for, with a
dish and an aerial, which is what the External Mast Walk and the satellite pass
are about. The skidoos are scooters, the skiway is the runway, and the station is
Aster Station.
