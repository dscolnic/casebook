# carrying_v2: put a syllabus number on every stop

Every stop in this campaign names a concept in its own words. The game needs
the course's own entry instead, because the sequencing checks grade *when* each
idea is first taught and they can only do that against one fixed list. Your
words are not being replaced — the tag stays in the bible; this adds the
number beside it.

The course is **AP Environmental Science, all nine units, on an inhabited island where every budget closes: ecosystems, biodiversity, populations, earth systems, land and water use, energy, atmospheric and aquatic pollution, and global change**.

## The spine — the only 34 answers there are

 1. An ecosystem as a budget: what comes in, what leaves, what stays
 2. Food webs and trophic levels: who eats whom, and how much of it
 3. Energy transfer between levels, and why the top is thin
 4. The water cycle over a small island: rain in, runoff and abstraction out
 5. The carbon and nitrogen cycles, and where a farm interrupts them
 6. Species richness, and what an island holds that a mainland does not
 7. Island biogeography: area, isolation and the species a place can keep
 8. Ecological tolerance, and the range a population can actually live in
 9. Ecosystem services, and the ones nobody is billed for
10. Exponential growth, and reading a doubling time off a rate
11. Carrying capacity, and what happens on the way past it
12. Generalists and specialists, r-selected and K-selected
13. Age structure and the demographic transition, on a nineteen-child roll
14. Soil: horizons, texture and what a thin soil cannot do
15. A watershed, and what a catchment collects
16. An aquifer, and what abstracting faster than recharge does to it
17. Residence time: how long a store takes to turn over
18. The tragedy of the commons, and the rule that answers it
19. Maximum sustainable yield, and why it sits at half the stock
20. Agricultural methods, irrigation and the salt they leave behind
21. Integrated pest management against spraying on a calendar
22. An ecological footprint, and what a visitor adds to one
23. Renewable and non-renewable, and what the cable is bringing
24. Energy against power: a kilowatt is not a kilowatt-hour
25. Efficiency, and where the rest of the fuel goes
26. Capacity factor and intermittency: what a turbine gives on average
27. Point and non-point sources, and which one a pipe is
28. Concentration against load, and why a small pipe can matter
29. Dose makes the poison: exposure, body mass and a dose-response curve
30. Eutrophication: nutrients in, oxygen out
31. Greenhouse gases, and comparing them on one scale
32. A warming, acidifying sea, and what a reef does about it
33. Invasive species, and the four things that make one
34. Reading an environmental claim: what a number is about, and over what period
