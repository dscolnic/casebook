# changeover_v2: put a syllabus number on every stop

Every stop in this campaign names a concept in its own words. The game needs
the course's own entry instead, because the sequencing checks grade *when* each
idea is first taught and they can only do that against one fixed list. Your
words are not being replaced — the tag stays in the bible; this adds the
number beside it.

The course is **AP Macroeconomics, all six units, run out of a commandeered railway terminus during a currency changeover: scarcity and choice, indicators and the business cycle, aggregate demand and supply, the financial sector, stabilisation policy, and the open economy**.

 8. Inflation as a rate, and the difference between a level and a rate
 9. Nominal against real: taking the price change out of a figure
10. Unemployment: who is counted, and who is not in the labour force
11. Frictional, structural and cyclical unemployment
12. The business cycle, and where an output gap sits in it
13. Aggregate demand, and the four components that move it
14. Short-run aggregate supply, and what a cost shock does to it
15. Long-run aggregate supply as the economy’s capacity
16. The spending multiplier, and why one round is not the total
17. Demand-pull and cost-push inflation, and telling them apart
18. What counts as money, and the three things it has to do
19. Fractional reserve banking: a deposit is somebody else’s loan
20. The money multiplier, and what a reserve requirement caps
21. The money market: a rate that clears the demand for money
22. The loanable funds market, and where saving meets investment
23. Nominal and real interest rates, and who the difference falls on
24. Fiscal policy: spending and taxation as levers
25. Monetary policy: the three tools and what each one moves
26. Lags, and why a correct policy can arrive as the wrong one
27. The short-run trade-off between inflation and unemployment
28. Expectations, credibility, and why a promise is a policy instrument
29. The balance of payments: the current account and its mirror
30. An exchange rate as a price, and what moves it

32. Reading an economic statistic: what it counts, over what period, and revised how often
