# midway_v2: put a syllabus number on every stop

Every stop in this campaign names a concept in its own words. The game needs
the course's own entry instead, because the sequencing checks grade *when* each
idea is first taught and they can only do that against one fixed list. Your
words are not being replaced — the tag stays in the bible; this adds the
number beside it.

The course is **AP Physics 1, algebra-based: kinematics, dynamics, circular motion, energy, momentum, simple harmonic motion, torque and rotation, and fluids**.

## The spine — the only 36 answers there are

 1. Velocity against speed: direction is part of the quantity
 2. Acceleration as the rate a velocity changes, whichever part of it changes
 3. The constant-acceleration relations, and which one has no time in it
 4. Free fall: everything falls at the same rate whatever its mass
 5. Newton's second law as the definition of a net force
 6. Free-body thinking: one object, every force on it, nothing else
 7. The normal force, and what a rider actually feels
 8. Friction as a force along a surface, and the coefficient that bounds it
 9. Centripetal acceleration: v² over r, pointing at the centre
10. What supplies the centripetal force in each case — a rail, a chain, a seat
11. The conical pendulum: an angle set by speed and radius, not by mass
12. The vertical loop, and the limiting case where the rail stops pushing
13. Work as a force times a distance along it
14. Kinetic energy, and why the square matters
15. Gravitational potential energy as weight times height
16. Conservation of mechanical energy, and dividing the mass out of it
17. Friction as negative work, and measuring it as the gap between ideal and actual
18. Power as work each second, and what a motor rating is
19. Momentum, and its conservation in a collision
20. Elastic against inelastic: which quantity survives
21. Impulse: force times time, and why a longer stop is a gentler one
22. Torque as force times the perpendicular distance
23. Static equilibrium: forces and torques both adding to zero
24. Rotational inertia, and that where the mass is matters more than how much
25. Angular velocity, and turning revolutions a minute into a speed
26. Simple harmonic motion: a restoring force proportional to displacement
27. The small-angle approximation, and where it stops being true
28. The pendulum period, and the cancellation that removes the mass from it
29. Resonance and damping: driving an oscillator in step or out of it
30. Measuring g from a period, and why it belongs to the place
31. Pressure in a liquid: depth, not width
32. Pascal's principle, and the distance a hydraulic gives up
33. Buoyancy: a floating body displaces its own weight
34. Flow: continuity, and the outlet speed a head produces
35. Safety factor: what a part can take over what it is asked to take
36. Wind load, and why the last few metres a second cost the most
