# the_trial_v2: put a syllabus number on every stop

Every stop in this campaign names a concept in its own words. The game needs
the course's own entry instead, because the sequencing checks grade *when* each
idea is first taught and they can only do that against one fixed list. Your
words are not being replaced — the tag stays in the bible; this adds the
number beside it.

The course is **AP Statistics — experimental design and inference — carried through a running clinical trial, with the trial methodology of an introductory clinical epidemiology course**.

## The spine — the only 33 answers there are

 1. Why a comparison needs a control arm at all
 2. Randomisation: what chance assignment buys, and what it cannot buy
 3. Allocation concealment: protecting the moment of entry
 4. Blinding, and the several people it can fail for separately
 5. Block and stratified randomisation: balance in small numbers
 6. Eligibility criteria, and what widening them changes
 7. The recruitment funnel: screened, eligible, consented, entered
 8. The primary endpoint, named in advance, and everything else
 9. Hard endpoints against judged ones, and blinded adjudication
10. Measurement error, rater drift and inter-rater agreement
11. Surrogate outcomes, and what they stand in for
12. Protocol deviations: what they do to the comparison
13. Amendments and prespecification: a plan that can be dated
14. Sample size and statistical power
15. Event-driven trials, and information fraction as progress
16. Type I and type II error, and what α and β are for
17. Multiplicity: every extra look and every extra slice costs something
18. Subgroup analysis, and why a subgroup finding is a hypothesis
19. Interim analysis, alpha spending and stopping boundaries
20. Conditional power and futility
21. The monitoring committee and the firewall around it
22. Intention to treat against per-protocol
23. Missing data, and why it matters why it is missing
24. Effect measures: relative risk, absolute risk reduction, number needed to treat
25. Confidence intervals: what a point estimate does not say
26. p-values, and what significance is not
27. Safety monitoring: adverse events, seriousness and expedited reporting
28. Causality assessment, and why reporting does not wait for it
29. Unblinding an individual for their own care
30. Data cleaning, queries and the database lock
31. Reporting honestly: what a sentence claims beyond its evidence
32. External evidence, replication and how a new result is weighed
33. Consent, and the obligations a trial keeps to the people in it
