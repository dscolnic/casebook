ALAMOS — HIGH-SCHOOL-LEVEL CAMPAIGNS
Location and main storyline for every campaign written at high-school course level.
Generated 2026-09-04 from themes.json, each theme's theme.js opening card, GAMES.md and STORIES.md.

Scope: campaigns whose COURSE is high school (AP or senior-high) plus the twenty
Quick Discoveries (audience grade 9). Excluded and listed at the end: the college
games, the twelve junior (_ms) editions at grade 6, and Hospital Heroes at grade 2.

BLACKOUT                                        theme: blackout        grade 12
Course:   Senior-high / first-year electrical engineering — AC power,
          transmission, protection, load
Location: Calder Switching Station. A flat graded river plain, a switchyard of
          gantries and transformers, and two circuits of lattice towers walking
          off the map. The only steel skyline in the set.
Story:    You are the night system operator for the Calder grid. Four million
          people are on it and nearly all their power crosses one line. The
          coldest week of the winter starts tonight. Over fifteen shifts you
          write The Calder Winter Survival Plan: what reserve is real, how hard
          the line may be pushed, and how to restart from dark. A bad call puts
          homes, a kidney clinic and the valley pumps dark for hours.

THE TRIAL                                       theme: the_trial       grade 12
Course:   AP Statistics — experimental design and inference, carried through a
          running clinical trial
Location: One long floor of the CLARION-3 coordinating centre. The walk down it
          is distance from the patient: screening and infusion at the south end,
          then monitoring, the central lab, adjudication, and the data floor
          with the enrolment wall at the north. The corridor is a gradient in
          evidence.
Story:    CLARION-3 tests a new drug against usual care — 2,400 people at 31
          hospitals, and nobody running it may know which arm is ahead. You are
          the methods and operations lead. In fifteen days you build The
          Monitoring Board Pack; a board of outsiders reads it in three weeks
          and decides whether more people are asked to join.

THE TRIAL (HS edition)                          theme: the_trial_hs    grade 12
Course:   AP Statistics, all nine units (retarget of the clinical-epidemiology
          and biostatistics original — same place, same cast, new syllabus)
Location: As above — the CLARION-3 coordinating centre floor.
Story:    Same fortnight, told for the AP student: the independent monitoring
          board sits in three weeks and can stop the trial. Stop early on noise

HEADWATER                                       theme: headwater       grade 12
Course:   Calculus. Home of DERIVE — build a result line by line, each line
          naming the rule it used
Location: A five-storey tower in a gorge beside Ashfell Dam. The whole east side
          is one floor-to-soffit glazed screen onto the spillway; the hallways
          and stairs have no ceiling at all — you look up the shaft to the sky.
Story:    Ashfell is 88% full and nine days of rain are forecast for the high
          ground. Four villages sit below the wall, and a dry summer means every
          cubic metre released now may be missed later. You are the duty
          engineer: each morning you decide how much water goes out and you show
          the working. The gauges are hours old and the storage numbers still
          rest on a 2003 lake-bed survey. In fifteen days you leave behind
          release rules another engineer can trust.

WELLMERE                                        theme: seedbank        grade 12
Course:   AP Biology — heredity, population genetics, selection, plant
          energetics
Location: Wellmere seed bank and breeding station on Saltmere Point. The layout
          IS the syllabus and the quantity is isolation distance: the crossing
          block alone at the centre, an empty buffer, the increase ring, another
          buffer, the trial ring in arcs, and the compound out on the neck. Sea
          on three bearings; one causeway south to a gate — also the only
          bearing contamination can arrive on.
Story:    A wheat disease is two hundred kilometres east and the wind that would
          carry it comes in over the causeway. The programme has one gene that
          stands up to it, and one is not enough. You are Season Lead: what goes
          into the ground in the next three weeks goes in because you said so.
          Anything not sown waits a year, and the forty-one thousand samples in
          the vault do not wait well.

SAFETY FACTOR                                   theme: midway          grade 11
Course:   AP Physics 1, all eight units, taught through derivations
Location: Corbin Park — a closed lakeside amusement park three weeks before it
          tries to reopen. The rides are the areas: the drop tower is
          kinematics, the coaster is energy, the wheel is torque, the flume is
          fluids. A 26 m lift hill, a vertical loop, a 28 m Ferris wheel, a 45 m
          drop tower and a pirate ship make the skyline.
Story:    The county has shut every ride because nobody can prove the old
          settings are safe. Eleven notebooks hold the numbers and almost none
          of the physics behind them. You are the ride engineer; in fifteen days
          you decide which of seven rides reopen. Sign a bad limit and riders
          take the risk; refuse everything and the park loses the season. Rebuild
          the proof before your name goes on the Corbin Park Certificate.

RED SAND                                        theme: redsand         grade 12
Course:   AP Chemistry, back half — kinetics, equilibrium, electrochemistry,
          entropy and free energy, phase behaviour, colligative properties
Location: Arcadia Rise, a propellant plant on Mars — the only place in the set
          on another planet. Nine modules buried to the eaves in regolith along
          one graded track, an ascent vehicle on a pad four hundred metres past
          the last of them with a propellant gauge up its side that fills as the
          campaign does, six hundred solar panels, butterscotch sky. The
          geography is the process: walking the site walks one carbon atom
          through the whole plant, in order.
Story:    The rocket must leave when Mars and Earth line up. You lead the plant
          that makes fuel from Martian air and ground. Over fifteen key sols you
          fill A Full Tank — enough methane and oxygen for six people to fly
          home. If the tanks fall short the rocket stays, and the crew waits
          twenty-six months for another way home.

YELLOW BAY                                      theme: yellowbay       grade 12
Course:   AP Chemistry, structure half — atomic structure and photoelectron
          spectroscopy, Coulombic attraction and ionisation energy, periodic

Location: Ardley Fab 7, a chip plant with its own world: two parallel gowned

          investigation assumed one.)

SIGHTLINE                                       theme: sightline       grade 12
Course:   AP Psychology, all five units — sensation and perception, memory,
          development and learning, social psychology, health through procedure
Location: A 1960s telephone exchange with the switch floor taken out: one hall

GROUND TRUTH                                    theme: groundtruth     grade 12
Course:   AP Physics C: Electricity & Magnetism, all five units, as ten
          derivations — Gauss, the potential integral, capacitance, stored

Location: Station 12, a rocket-triggered lightning research station on a coastal

Story:    Station 12 makes lightning on purpose: a rocket carries a thin wire

          You are the measurements lead. In fifteen days find how the strike
          reached that trailer and write the rule that gets all six crew inside
          before the sky becomes dangerous. Until you can defend both, the
          station cannot keep firing.

PART 2 — THE QUICK DISCOVERIES (grade 9; nine stops, three levels, one sitting)
================================================================================
Written for grade 9 rather than AP. Every card teaches its method before it asks
anything; every campaign is one historical discovery, played from inside it. No
warm-up runs and no second day; dayNoun is 'Level'.

01 THE DOUBLE HELIX                    qd_dna         Biology
   Where: the Structural Biology Unit — four benches off one spine.
   Story: nobody knows what the instructions for life are made of. Four benches
   have each measured something true about the same substance and none can say
   its shape. Three shapes are standing this morning; one gets defended in print
   on Friday, and no shape comes off the wall until somebody names the clue it
   breaks.

02 THE ACCELERATING UNIVERSE           qd_accel       Astronomy / cosmology
   Where: Cerro Vela Survey Operations, a mountain survey site.
   Story: forty-two exploding stars have come back too faint — further away than
   a universe of matter can allow. Either something nobody has found is pushing
   the universe apart faster and faster, or it is dust, bad calibration, or a
   ruler built wrong. You are the analyst; the sealed sample opens Wednesday.

03 THE ATOMIC NUCLEUS                  qd_nucleus     Physics
   Where: the scattering laboratory.
   Story: ten alpha particles in ten thousand come back out of a gold sheet the
   way they went in — impossible on the accepted, smeared-out atom. Something in
   that gold hits far harder than any atom should. You write the number on
   Monday; five people spend the week counting flashes in the dark on it.

04 PLATE TECTONICS                     qd_tectonics   Earth science
   Where: the Global Survey Section, with a second wall locked until this morning.
   Story: the Atlantic coasts match — fossils, rock belts, glacial scratches —
   and for forty years nobody could name a force big enough to move a continent.
   Then the ships came back with sea-floor rock ages, magnetic traces and forty
   years of undersea quakes. One story that covers all of it, by Wednesday.

05 THE HIGGS BOSON                     qd_higgs       Particle physics
   Where: the collider analysis floor.
   Story: one slot on the wall chart is empty, and filling it makes the reason
   anything has mass measurable. The thing falls apart the moment it is made,
   leaving a few extra events at one mass among billions that look identical. A
   hall is booked for Thursday; the sentence on that screen is quoted for twenty
   years.

06 THE BENDING OF STARLIGHT            qd_eclipse     Physics (outdoors)
   Where: an eclipse expedition camp on a plain with a clear horizon.
   Story: two theories say starlight bends past the Sun and they differ by a
   factor of two — the larger number means space itself is bent. The bend shows
   only right beside the Sun, where the Sun is far too bright. The Moon covers it
   for five minutes next month. One mount, two lenses, no second try, and the
   shift is a third of the width of a hair.

07 THE FIRST EXOPLANET                 qd_exo         Astronomy
   Where: the planet search programme's working floor.
   Story: twenty nights of spectra show a star's whole forest of lines sliding
   one way then the other, a few parts in ten million. If the puller is another
   star this is a plain binary and the season is wasted; if it is small enough,
   it is a planet round somebody else's sun and nobody has ever measured one. A
   class goes on the sheet by Thursday and onto the discovery paper.

08 GRAVITATIONAL WAVES                 qd_ligo        Physics
   Where: the interferometer corner station.
   Story: at four in the morning something crossed both instruments at once —
   a fifth of a second, climbing in pitch, then gone. A lorry, a cracked weld,
   or two black holes a billion light years off stretching a four-kilometre arm
   by a thousandth of a proton's width. Nobody calls the second site until you
   can say what the shape is.

09 THE COSMIC MICROWAVE BACKGROUND     qd_cmb         Cosmology (outdoors)
   Where: a hilltop horn-antenna site above a road.
   Story: a hiss about three degrees above absolute zero arrives from every
   direction and will not go away. Air, ground and road are already subtracted.
   Everyone on the hill thinks the receiver is broken — and if it is not, the
   whole sky glows at one temperature and nobody here can name a thing that
   would do that. (The first explanation offered was pigeons.)

10 THE EXPANDING UNIVERSE              qd_hubble      Astronomy
   Where: a mountain observatory's working floor, with the plate vault across
   the corridor.
   Story: every faint nebula anybody has a spectrum of is running away, and the
   list has stayed a list because none of them carries a distance. Now the vault
   has a way to measure distance. If speed and distance go together, only two
   answers are left: we sit at the middle of something enormous, or the whole
   thing is getting bigger. Hubble wants a line on the plot by Wednesday.

11 PENICILLIN                          qd_penicillin  Microbiology (1928)
   Where: the Inoculation Department.
   Story: a plate that should have gone into the wash has a ring on it with
   nothing growing inside and a wind-blown mould colony at the centre. If
   something is coming off that mould, an untreatable infection has an answer;
   if the ring is a dry patch, four benches waste a week. Florey lets no claim
   stay on the wall without the measurement under it — and a week of everything
   the department can make comes to three days of treatment for one adult.

12 GERM THEORY OF DISEASE              qd_germ        Medicine / public health
   Where: Marlow Fields — an 1854 Victorian court, outdoors, with a standpipe.
   Story: sixty-six people in one court are dead or dying while houses fifty
   yards away are untouched. Some blame the smell, some the weather, some the
   poor themselves. If one thing reaches these people by a nameable route,
   blocking it would stop the illness and change how doctors work everywhere.
   The vestry votes Friday on the pump handle; forty households draw from it.

13 THE LAWS OF INHERITANCE             qd_mendel      Genetics
   Where: the abbey garden — trial beds, outdoors.
   Story: cross a yellow-seeded pea with a green and every seed is yellow; sow
   those and a quarter come up green again. Something green rode through a plant
   that never showed it. If the counts follow a rule, inheritance is arithmetic;
   if they wander, it is a blending nobody will get a grip on. Mendel has one
   season and two hundred and fifty beds and will not sow until the trait is
   settled.

14 CRISPR GENE EDITING                 qd_crispr      Molecular biology (2012)
   Where: the Genome Editing Institute.
   Story: forty bacterial genomes carry the same odd shape — one short repeat,
   and between every pair a different piece, one of which matches a virus
   exactly over thirty bases. Either these cells keep a written record of what
   attacked them, and a machine told what to find by a copy can be told to find
   anything — or the match is luck and a month is wasted. One sequencing run on
   Friday, nineteen sites worth worrying about, depth for four.

15 OXYGEN AND COMBUSTION               qd_oxygen      Chemistry (1775)
   Where: the pneumatic laboratory.
   Story: ten grams of lead heated in open air leave 12.7 grams of powder.
   Burning is supposed to take things away. If the gain is real, something
   joined the lead while it burnt and every account of fire has to be rewritten;
   if the balance is wrong, a season is wasted. Lavoisier will not write a figure
   he cannot account for, the academy pays for one more experiment, and six
   people disagree about which.

16 PERIODIC TABLE                      qd_periodic    Chemistry (1869)
   Where: the Chemistry Institute — sixty-three numbered drawers and a long
   table.
   Story: lithium, sodium and potassium behave the same and weigh nothing like
   each other, so chemistry stays a list to be learned by heart. Lay the elements
   out so behaviour keeps coming round and something underneath is causing it —
   and the layout can say what belongs in a place nobody has filled. The
   proceedings go to press Friday: either figures for elements nobody has, or
   nothing at all.

17 STROOP EFFECT                       qd_stroop      Cognitive psychology (1935)
   Where: the reaction-time laboratory.
   Story: print GREEN in red ink, ask for the ink colour, and people stumble
   though they know the answer. If a well-drilled habit runs whether anybody
   wants it or not, attention is a race between two answers rather than a
   choice; if the stumble is a fiddly card, a week of counting is wasted.
   Forty-two people booked, two numbers a session.

18 FALSE MEMORIES                      qd_memory      Cognitive psychology
   Where: the Memory and Testimony Unit (Sightline's twelve-room shape).
   Story: read fifteen words that all point at a sixteenth, leave that one out,
   and more than half the room reports hearing it — not guessing, not lying. If
   memory can be built out of meaning alone, what you ask a witness matters as
   much as what they saw. Two hundred people booked, one clause of one question
   different between them, and nine officers arrive Friday wanting to know what
   to change.

19 KEYNES AND THE GREAT DEPRESSION     qd_keynes      Macroeconomics (1933)
   Where: the Economic Advisory Building.
   Story: a fifth of the country is out of work and staying that way, factories
   stand still, people want jobs — and prices have barely moved, which is the
   wrong way round. Maybe an economy can settle in a bad place and stay there,
   and something could shift it. Keynes wants the fall traced arrow by arrow
   before anybody argues about prices. The report goes out Friday; two million
   are out of work while it is written.

20 BANK RUNS                           qd_bankrun     Economics and finance
   Where: floors two to five of Marrable House, on interiorTower — one footprint,
   a lift the only way between floors.
   Story: a queue on the pavement below the window. Nothing has changed since
   yesterday: the loans are good, the borrowers are paying, the book covers the
   deposits. But ten units of cash are in the building and thirty-two have been
   asked for by noon. Can a sound bank be closed by the order people arrive in?
   If so, the thing that fails is the arrangement, not the bank.

PART 3 — BORDERLINE: grade-12 reading, course level not stated as AP

Included for completeness; both sit at the school/undergraduate boundary and each
also ships a grade-6 junior edition.

ICE CORE                                        theme: icecore         grade 12
Course:   Earth and environmental science — the measurement half of a
          palaeoclimate course
Location: Vestri Dome: a deep-drilling camp on a high polar plateau. Six modules
          on legs beside a groomed route, flag lines both sides, a trench with a
          tower over it, a stake array two kilometres out. The flattest, emptiest
          horizon in the set.
Story:    Vestri and Skarv drilled ice four hundred kilometres apart; both cores
          cover one cold spell and the two records disagree. You are the season
          science lead, so the signing is yours. In fifteen days you sign the
          Vestri Record: what the ice measured, which clocks turn that into
          dates, and where the proof stops. If the gap is really the two labs and
          you call it climate, every record tied to these cores carries it for
          years.

AFTERSHOCK                                      theme: aftershock      grade 12
Course:   Seismology and structural engineering
Location: Kestrel Bay — two places on one map that the same earthquake treated
          differently: Upper Town on a granite bench, mostly standing; the Flats
          on hydraulic fill, liquefied, with sand fans and a block sitting over
          at eight degrees. Between them the Kestrel Fault broke the surface and
          the player walks a 1.8 m scarp with a road and a fence offset across
          it. The site-effect lesson is drawn on the ground.
Story:    Three days after a magnitude 6.8 quake, four hundred homes still sleep
          in halls. Every door has a card: green to use, yellow for limits, red
          for nobody. You are the building safety coordinator and aftershocks
          keep coming. In fifteen days you sign the Placard Register — what each
          building may be used for, and what proves it. Too bold and a child goes
          back into a wall that will not hold; too shy and a school stays shut.
