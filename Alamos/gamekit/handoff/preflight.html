Alamos · authoring a revised bundle

Bundle Pre-Flight

Twelve books landed across two bundles, and eleven of them were refused by the importer on first contact — nothing written, no gate run, the campaign still shipping its old content. This page is for whoever writes the next one, and it assumes you cannot run the checks: every rule below is stated as something to satisfy while writing the YAML, and every one is verifiable by reading what you wrote.

The eight things to check by reading, before you hand the book over

In this order, because the first three decide whether anything is written at all. Each maps to a section below.

  1. Every takesAsRead: entry is a prerequisite of that stop's own concept: — not a related idea, and never the concept itself. This one defect accounts for 25 of the refusals.
  2. Every concept: is copied from the syllabus, character for character — or given as its number, which cannot drift.
  3. Every instrument's own data lets it be lost, won and graded — a keyed answer that is one of its options, a board a do-nothing run fails, a belt bank of 24+ split 8/8 or better with names of three words or fewer.
  4. Every apostrophe inside a single-quoted string in theme.js is escaped — \'. An unescaped one means the file does not parse, and no gate in the repo looks for it.
  5. A compound diagnosis pack's stop carries its answerText as the hypothesis labels joined by " + ", verbatim. The pack's own answer: field is read by nothing.
  6. The opening card is five sentences, 70–180 words, one paragraph, four beats — and names the thing the campaign builds.
  7. Each day's stake plus briefing is four sentences or fewer, and says what the player decides in the "Today you …" shape.
  8. Each warm-up card's why is one sentence of twelve words or more — the run cards are read standing in the world with the run about to start.

Nothing here needs a terminal. If you do have one, the single most valuable command is a dry import — it writes nothing and reports every blocker at once — followed by a syntax check on the manifest and a play-through. Those three catch everything in the first two sections of this page.

Blockers — the importer writes nothing

Ranked by how often they actually occurred. None of these is a content gate: while any one of them stands, import-book.mjs prints the problems and exits without touching a file, so the campaign ships its previous content and every green check is about the old version.

A takesAsRead the concept is not built out of

25 across two bundles
every book that failed
The rule
takesAsRead: names the syllabus concepts a stop is entitled to assume, and it is checked against the prerequisites of that stop's own concept — not against the syllabus at large. A declaration the claim does not need is one nothing will ever check, so the importer refuses it rather than let a dead line sit in the book.
The three shapes it takes
A concept that is on the syllabus but is not a prerequisite of this stop. A concept that is not on the syllabus at all, usually a paraphrase of one that is. And the commonest: the stop declaring its own concept — takesAsRead: Kinematics on a Kinematics stop, which the error reports as "is not something "Kinematics" is built out of (it needs "Vectors and components")".
Before you write the line
Which concept does this stop claim, and what does tools/syllabus.js say that concept needs? The answer to the second question is the only legal content of takesAsRead.
The fix, when it is already written
The error names the prerequisite the stop actually needs. Swap to that, or delete the line. Deleting is always safe — the declaration is an optimisation for conceptOrder, not a requirement.

A concept: that is not on the syllabus

7 in one bundle
4 in one book
The rule
A stop's concept: must be a syllabus entry's exact title, or its number. Anything else is unresolvable, and an unresolved concept means nothing checks the claim the card makes.
What it looks like
Always a plausible paraphrase, never nonsense — "Site response — one earthquake, different local ground motion" for "Site effect: soft ground amplifies"; "Response spectrum: turning a measured record into period-specific demand" for "Natural period, and resonance between building and ground". The author knew the physics and re-titled it.
Before you write the line
Is this string copied from tools/syllabus.js, or typed from memory? Copy it.
Cheap defence
Give the number instead of the title. A number cannot drift from the syllabus and cannot be paraphrased.

An instrument whose own data makes it unplayable

6 in one bundle
4 formats
The rule
Every live instrument is simulated at import. A board that cannot be lost, cannot be won, or cannot be graded is refused — it would render perfectly and ask nothing.
The four that actually occurred
TRIGGER — consequenceLimit sat exactly on the top of its own stream, so the needle could neither cross it nor stay under it. It must be strictly inside the range the stream reaches. HOLD — the do-nothing run stayed inside the band for 100% of the run against an 80% pass, so a player who never touched the control passed. The importer integrates the disturbance rate steps in closed form and tells you the worst drift. BELT — a 12-item bank (24 minimum, because the bank is shuffled and only part of it is played), a 5/7 bin split (8 minimum each), and item names over three words, which are unreadable on a moving tile. DIAGNOSIS — the keyed answer was not one of the candidates, and grading compares labels.
Before you author a panel
What does a player who does nothing score? If the answer is "a pass", the board is decoration.

Invisible to every gate

Both of these passed npm run check and would have shipped. One surfaced when a PDF build printed a parse error; the other when a play-through found an ungradeable stop. This is the class the repo's own tripwire is about: a clean check is not evidence.

An unescaped apostrophe in a single-quoted manifest string

2 in one file
caught by a PDF build
What happened
A revised theme.js carried 'Then Farrow's independent checks…' inside single quotes. The file does not parse. make-cardbook reported Unexpected identifier 's' and nothing in npm run check pointed at that file, because a theme whose manifest will not load is a theme most gates skip rather than fail.
How to avoid it while writing
Prefer double quotes for any string containing an apostrophe, or escape it as \'. The strings that carry this risk are the ones with prose in them — opening, ending, and delivery.what — and English prose about people is full of possessives.

A diagnosis pack graded against the wrong field

1 stop
caught by smokeCampaign
What happened
A DIAGNOSIS stop referencing a pack was ungradeable. The pack carried its own answer: lotvariation — a hypothesis key — and applyPack in engine/content/normalize.js never reads it. It grades against the stop's answerText, split on " + ", matched against hypothesis labels. The stop's answerText was a paraphrase of the two labels rather than the labels.
The rule to write down
For a compound pack, the stop's answerText must be the hypothesis labels joined by " + ", verbatim. The pack's own answer: field is read by nothing.
Why no gate saw it
Only a play-through of every stop catches an ungradeable one, and the importer's own DIAGNOSIS check does not run on the pack path — it validates the pack exists and stops there. Reading the stop is the defence: if its answerText is not two labels from the pack's hypotheses with " + " between them, it cannot be graded.

The trade-offs that bit the third bundle

The third bundle was written to bring the reading level down, and it did — one campaign's sixteen unavoidable cards went from mean grade 9.0 to 4.5. Three things broke on the way, and all three are the same mistake: a card cut until the number was right, past the point where the card still did its job. The caps below are the shape to write to instead.

The three card caps, and which one applies where

one splitter
three settings
The caps
They step down with how little of the reader's attention the card is entitled to. The opening card gets five sentences — it is read once and carries four beats. A day card — the stake plus any briefing that adds to it — gets four, because it is read before every one of fifteen days. A warm-up card gets one: the player is standing in the world with a run about to start and the card is over the top of it, so the only thing that has to survive is why this run is happening today.
The floors that pull the other way
The opening card also has a 70-word floor, and a warm-up why has a twelve-word floor. So a warm-up card is one sentence of twelve words or more — a narrower target than it looks, and 276 of the 308 cards in the repo miss it today. Write to the beat, then count; do not cut to the number and hope the beat survived.
Why they cannot disagree
All three are counted by one function in engine/dev/dayCard.mjs. A decimal is not a full stop, a colon is not a full stop, and a paragraph break is not a sentence boundary — so "it holds 3.9 tonnes today" is one sentence at every cap.

A grade bought by deleting course vocabulary

1 campaign
gated, and it fired
What the gate said
plainQuestions: "syllables/word FELL: 1.669 → 1.575 — a grade bought by deleting course vocabulary is the one thing the pass forbids." Three of its six figures are gated and spw is the one that may not fall, precisely because a falling syllable count is what vocabulary deletion looks like from the outside.
The rule it enforces
Never delete the official term to lower a reading level. Name it and gloss it on the spot — "its rate — how fast the gas reacts". A student who never meets the word cannot read their own textbook.
Before you simplify a card
Did the grade fall because the sentences got shorter, or because a term went missing? The number cannot tell you. Read the card.

An opening card cut under its floor

2 campaigns
55 and 58 words
What happened
Both openings now clear the five-sentence cap and fail the 70-word floor instead. One also lost its closing stake — it ends on "Build the Return Checklist and make each call before air, power, or fuel…", which carries no number, no clock and nobody in it.
The shape to hit
Five sentences, 70 to 180 words, one paragraph, and four beats: what has happened and to whom; the job stated as authority ("You are the …, which means …"); the clock or the argument; and last, what it costs, in a line with a number, a clock or a person in it. The cap and the floor are both real and they are eleven words apart at five sentences — write to the beats, then count.

The delivery's name dropped out of the opening

4 campaigns
2 of them regressions
What happened
Every course campaign builds one named thing, a piece a day, on a board in a room the player can walk into. The opening card has to name it. Two of the shortened openings dropped the name that the previous version carried, so the player is told the stakes and not the job.
The cheap version of the beat
"In fifteen days you sign the <named thing>: what X, what Y, and what nobody has checked yet." One clause, and it satisfies the delivery gate and the third beat at once.

Ratchet etiquette

Most gates in this repo are ratcheted against a debt file, and the mechanism cuts both ways — which surprises people the first time a fix shows up as a failure.

A fixed card fails until it is re-banked

56 lines in one campaign
What it looks like
"reasoncoverage-debt.json lists this stop as missing a reason, and it now has one — delete the line." Forty-five of these appeared at once when a revised book wrote the reason lines a debt file was still recording as owed. They read like defects and are the opposite.
The fix, and it is not yours
Re-banking is a maintainer step (checkStory.mjs <theme> --write-debt), not an authoring one. What you can do is say in the handover which cards you fixed, so whoever re-banks knows the failures are cleared debt rather than new defects.
The catch worth knowing
--write-debt is per theme and all-or-nothing: it banks current gaps as well as clearing fixed ones. On the third bundle it absorbed a genuine regression — fifteen stakes that had lost their "Today you …" clause were banked in the same pass that cleared forty-five fixes. Read the before/after counts it prints rather than trusting the exit code.
What never to do
Delete a debt line to quiet a gate. A line whose card now passes fails on its own and names itself for deletion; that is the mechanism working. Deleting a line whose card still fails converts a tracked gap into an untracked one.
