The Nine-Agent Pass

684 gate findings across the nine campaigns of the second revised bundle. One agent per campaign, working its own book and nothing else, in an order chosen so that the passes do not undo one another. The pilot is one campaign, because the risk in this plan is not collisions — it is nine agents each writing plausible prose that satisfies a number.

What each agent owns

One campaign, and exactly two files. Every other path an agent touches is derived from these by a tool it runs itself.

Two files, and the reason it is only two

  • books/<book>.yml — every card edit. Never themes/<id>/content/* by hand: bookParity requires the book to regenerate what ships, so a hand-edit there is a defect that surfaces two commits later.
  • themes/<id>/theme.js — the opening card, the ending card and delivery.what. Four of the failing gates live in this file, not in the book.

Then the agent runs import-book.mjs for its own theme, which writes only themes/<id>/content/. Those three paths are disjoint across all nine agents.

Why no worktrees Because the paths are disjoint, the same tree is safe — which saves nine merges and nine re-imports. Isolation buys nothing here; the discipline below is what buys safety.

The three shared things that would corrupt

This is the load-bearing part of the plan, and one of the three is not obvious.

What agents may run freely

Per-theme gate runs — conceptVisible, plainQuestions, checkStory, probeQuestions, smokeCampaign and the rest — are read-only and take a theme name. They are parallel-safe, and each agent is expected to measure itself continuously rather than at the end.

The work order inside a campaign

These passes are not independent, and one ordering is materially better than the others.

  1. Mechanical. Glossary entries for undefined terms, a job beside each first-named person (checkNames, warmupOrder), the delivery's name into the opening card, the over-long board caption, warm-up cards down to one sentence. No judgement calls, and it closes whole gates.
  2. Concept visibility. Put the concept's own term into the question or one option. Before the reading-level pass, because this step raises course vocabulary — doing it afterwards means fighting the pass that has just removed words.
  3. Reading level. plainCards, plainQuestions, and the per-card grade ceiling — now operating on cards that already carry their term, so a grade cannot be bought by deleting it.
  4. Story shape. The reason line under every call, the "Today you …" clause, stake length, and the three card caps.
  5. Curriculum order. conceptOrder, equationOrder, takesAsRead. Last, because steps 2 to 4 move cards around.
The reason step 2 precedes step 3 Bring Them Home's plainQuestions gate fired this session on exactly this: grade 8.21 → 6.13 with syllables per word falling 1.669 → 1.575. A grade bought by deleting the course's vocabulary is the one thing the accessibility pass forbids, and the gate that watches for it is the one that caught it.

The brief carries constraints, not just targets

Each agent receives its campaign's handoff document, its baseline gate output, the Bundle Pre-Flight page and the alamos-accessibility rules. Plus four hard rules, because each is a way to make a number improve while the content gets worse.

  • Never delete an official term to lower a grade. Name it and gloss it on the spot. plainQuestions gates spw against falling for precisely this.
  • Do not make the concept keyword the giveaway. Adding a term to one option can hand the answer away by length or wording; probeQuestions LEAK and ECHO catch that, and the agent re-runs it after every batch.
  • Never touch a debt file. Report what was fixed; re-banking is serialized.
  • Do not commit. The deliverable is a diff plus a before/after gate table.

Pilot, then fan out

Nine agents on an unvalidated brief is the expensive mistake. One campaign first.

What is serialized, and why it is not delegated

  • Before: freeze the shared files, snapshot a per-campaign baseline for every gate.
  • After each agent: re-run its gates rather than trusting its report — specifically probeQuestions and plainQuestions, the two an agent can game without meaning to. A report is a claim; the gate is the evidence.
  • At the end: one --write-debt pass, the cardbook PDFs, the shots, one full npm run check, one commit.

The honest risk

Nine campaigns at roughly fifty cards each is the largest content job this repo has taken on, and it is genuinely expensive. The pilot exists so the price can be read off one campaign rather than nine.

The risk that matters is not file conflicts — the disjoint-paths argument handles those. It is 684 findings closed and the cards worse to read: nine agents each producing prose that satisfies a measurement. Two things guard against it, and neither is a gate. The first is that step 2 precedes step 3, so vocabulary goes in before reading level comes down. The second is that every agent's numbers are re-measured rather than accepted.

Where the numbers come from

Every count is the gate's own output against the shipped content on branch deep-watch-integration, 1 September 2026, after the second revised bundle landed and after conceptVisible and the warm-up card cap went in.
