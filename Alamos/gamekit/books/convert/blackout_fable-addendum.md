## The equations this course may compute

Substitution into a one-line formula, with numbers chosen to stay manageable.
The formula is there to show where the answer comes from, not to be an
arithmetic obstacle.

| Equation | What it is | Taught after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `V = IR` | Ohm's law, on a conductor and on a whole network | — |
| `P = IV, and P = I²R` | power delivered, and the loss that never arrives | `V = IR` |
| `V_rms = V_peak / √2` | why an AC quantity needs saying which one you mean | — |
| `S = √(P² + Q²), pf = P/S` | apparent power and power factor | `P = IV, and P = I²R`, `V_rms = V_peak / √2` |
| `N₁/N₂ = V₁/V₂` | the transformer ratio, and why transmission is at high voltage | `P = IV, and P = I²R` |
| `P = √3 · V_L · I_L · cos φ` | three-phase power from line quantities | `P = IV, and P = I²R`, `S = √(P² + Q²), pf = P/S` |
| `df/dt = (P_gen − P_load) / 2H` | frequency as the running balance of supply and demand | — |
| `ΔV ≈ I(R cos φ + X sin φ)` | voltage drop along a line, and why reactive power moves it | `V = IR`, `S = √(P² + Q²), pf = P/S` |
| `peak = base peak + sensitivity × degrees below` | how far cold moves an evening peak | — |
| `E = Pt` | energy from power over time — the kilowatt-hour | `P = IV, and P = I²R` |
| `η = E_out / E_in` | round-trip efficiency of storage | — |
