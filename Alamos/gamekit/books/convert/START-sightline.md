# Sightline — instructions for this game's pass

Hand over three files together:

```
books/convert/sightline-stops.jsonl            the 45 stops, editable
books/convert/sightline-stops.manifest.json    course, curriculum, equations, groups
books/interactions/CONVERSION_BRIEF.md         the nineteen formats and every rule
```

This note is the game-specific half. The brief is the general half and everything
in it still applies — the reading level rule, the takeaway rule, the minimums, the
checklist in §8. Nothing here overrides it.

## What this game is

The Hallam Conviction Integrity Unit has fifteen working days to report on
*State v. Elias Ward*: a 2019 robbery conviction resting on one eyewitness
identification and nothing beside it, seven years served of fourteen. The player
is the review analyst — not a lawyer, not a detective, the person who has to say
what the evidence is worth. Six areas: Perception & the Scene, Memory &
Interview, Identification Procedure, Stress · Sleep & the Body, Influence & the
Room, Testing & Records.

The place is one hall of a converted telephone exchange with the Ferrier Street
corner rebuilt across the south end of it at 1:1, and the identification
distances let into the corridor floor. **Walking the hall is walking the
sightline.** Any rewrite that stops mentioning where the player is standing loses
the only thing this game has that the other sixteen do not.

**The argument the campaign turns on.** Ines Baptiste holds that the procedure is
already unrecoverable, that every month spent proving it a second way is a month
a man serves, and that almost none of the review's findings need the witness at
all. Ren Okada holds that the November 2019 interview is the last account of that
night nobody has influenced, that it is perishable, and that using it up is a
decision that cannot be taken twice. **Baptiste is right on day 4. Okada is right
on day 12.** Neither is a straw man. Preserve both voices exactly.

Audience: **grade 12**, AP Psychology, all five units. The subject's vocabulary is
not negotiable — "functional size", "source monitoring", "criterion" are the
terms — so the lever on reading level is sentence length.

## What may not change

These are the campaign, not the prose. A rewrite that moves any of them breaks
the arc, the checks, or both.

**The facts the reversal is built on.** The case summary says 22 m; Bekele's
survey says 34.2 m, confirmed against the shop's lease plan to 0.2 m; the sketch
the summary was read off is marked not to scale; Cardoza has put herself in the
same place in three accounts. Column 4471 carries an 1,800 candela lantern 12 m
from the doorway, and the utility's fault ticket has it out from 2 to 21 November
2019 — so the corner had about 2 lux of shop-window spill, not the 12.5 the lamp
would have given. The robbery is 14 November 2019 at 21:40. The first full
interview is 18 November; the Register runs Ward's photograph on the 19th; the
array is 4 December; the "absolutely certain" statement is 26 December, and the
array form itself says "maybe, number four".

**The numbers that are computed.** 0.063 m ÷ 22 m = 0.0029 rad · 1800 ÷ 12² =
12.5 lux · 40 ÷ 26 = 1.54 effective members · 0.0003 × 34 = 0.0102 m · 0.52 −
(−0.52) = 1.04 · 0.00025 × 15 = 0.00375 · (51 − 42) ÷ 7 = 1.29. Seven of seven
syllabus equations are computed today and none is merely mentioned; a conversion
that drops an estimate has to put the same arithmetic somewhere else on the same
day or earlier, or `equationOrder` and `syllabusEquations` will say so.

**The ending the game refuses to write.** Day 15 sorts four claims by what stands
behind each, and the fourth — that Ward was not the man — is matched to "not
established by anything in this review". Day 9's IDENT stop makes the same
refusal in advance. The game never says who committed the robbery, and a rewrite
that lets it is the one change that would make this a different game.

**The cast, by role.** Baptiste (Unit Director), Okada (Memory Scientist), Frey
(Vision Scientist), Ilori (Identification Procedures Adviser), Raghunathan
(Psychophysiologist), Weill (Psychometrician), Solomon (Case Analyst), Bekele
(Reconstruction Technician), Loomis (Retired Detective, who ran the 2019 array
and followed the policy of her year), Oyelaran (Clinical Psychologist), Ntuli
(Deliberation Study Coordinator), Achterberg (Records Custodian). Off-roster and
named in prose only: Alma Cardoza the witness, Deniz Aktaş the shop assistant,
Elias Ward. **Loomis is not a villain** and no rewrite may make her one.

**The safety frame.** No gore, no victim depicted; the crime reaches the player as
a file, a floor plan and a lux reading. The player never interrogates anybody,
never diagnoses anybody, and never assesses a real person's credibility — every
clinical stop is reached through procedure and record.

## What to convert, and what suits this game

Six to nine conversions, at most one per day, on the eight days that have none.
Formats that fit the material, with the stop they would replace:

* **VALUE** — day 14 already asks what a measurement would change; a second one
  suits day 11 (what a properly dated confidence statement would have been worth).
* **TRIGGER** — day 5, the may-not-be-present caution: write the rule before the
  array is shown.
* **ATTEST** again is available on day 15 (four claims, what backs each) but the
  CASEBOOK there already does that job; prefer something else.
* **BALANCE** — day 6, the dated documents: close the ledger on when each detail
  entered the account and find the one with no source on the night.
* **PROPAGATE** — day 9, which input width dominates: the distance, the
  illuminance or the viewing time.
* **STRESS** — day 12, the admission against the range of states it could have
  been given in.
* **VERIFY** — day 2, predict what the volunteers will resolve at that range, run
  it, measure, compare.
* **CONTROL / TRACE / CLOUD / INJECT are already used** — a second instance is
  allowed but a new format is worth more.

**DERIVE does not suit this game.** Its subject is algebraic manipulation and
this course has none; the arithmetic here is one line at a time.

## The four things it will get wrong

1. **Reordering options.** They are applied by position. Rewording in place is
   fine; moving one re-keys the question to whatever lands in that slot, and
   every check still passes.
2. **A `BALLPARK` with no runnable `formula`.** A prompt and a worked solution in
   prose is not an estimate — the panel returns NaN. It needs `labels`, `values`,
   `correct`, `slots`, `template`, `formula`, `target`, `tolerance`, `units`, at
   least one distractor tile, and no tile that *is* the answer.
3. **Long sentences, not hard words.** The gate is Flesch–Kincaid, so sentence
   length dominates. Four days already sit at 10.0 or above against an audience
   of 12; two grades over fails outright.
4. **A pass mark that is a sentence, or a number nothing can reach.** Every
   threshold, target, tolerance and pass mark is a number, and the arithmetic has
   to work at both ends — the cheap move falls short, the full set of right moves
   clears. `trap_is_satisfied_by` has to state both.

One more, particular to this game: **the keyed answer must not be the longest
option.** It was 49% of the way there on the first draft and was rebalanced to
23%; a batch of rewrites that puts the reason back in the key undoes that, and
`answerShape` will fail the game.

## The prompt

> You are editing one educational game — an AP Psychology campaign for grade 12
> — to convert some of its multiple-choice stops into instrument panels and to
> tighten its prose.
>
> I have uploaded three files:
>
> 1. `CONVERSION_BRIEF.md` — the brief: the nineteen instrument formats, the book
>    block each needs, and the trap each has to clear. Read §4b and §8 before
>    writing anything.
> 2. `START-sightline.md` — this game specifically: what it is, the argument it
>    turns on, the facts and numbers that may not change, the fifteen days as they
>    stand, and which days have no instrument.
> 3. `sightline-stops.jsonl` — the forty-five stops, one JSON object per line.
>    Every field the brief lists as editable is yours to rewrite.
>
> Work in five batches of three days, starting with days 1–3. For each batch
> return only the JSONL rows for those days, edited in place — same ids, same
> field names, same option order. Stop after each batch and wait for me to say
> "next".
>
> Across the whole game convert six to nine stops into instruments, at most one
> per day, and only on the days START-sightline.md lists as carrying none. Take
> variety in the formats and prefer one this game does not already use. Every
> converted row carries its own `data` block with real numbers, and the trap
> arithmetic stated at both ends in `trap_is_satisfied_by`.
>
> Do not change the facts listed under "What may not change". Do not let the game
> conclude who committed the robbery.
>
> After the last batch, write the prose summary the brief asks for in §8: your
> editorial opinion of the game, the questions that were too easy and what gave
> them away, a day sheet, which curriculum concepts are thin, and what you
> deliberately did not change.

## When it comes back

```sh
cd gamekit
node tools/check-conversions.mjs sightline returned.jsonl
node tools/apply-conversions.mjs sightline returned.jsonl --dry
node tools/apply-conversions.mjs sightline returned.jsonl
node tools/import-book.mjs books/sightline.yml sightline --verify
npm run check sightline
npm run drive sightline
```

`check-conversions` normalises field names that came back close but not exact,
which is why the prompt says nothing about getting them right. It will not invent
a number, so a row whose trap fails is rejected with the arithmetic quoted.

If what you want is a pure copy-edit rather than a format pass, the other channel
is `node tools/export-copy.mjs sightline` → `books/copy/sightline-copy.jsonl`,
one row per editable sentence with the structure stripped out, applied back with
`tools/apply-copy.mjs`.
