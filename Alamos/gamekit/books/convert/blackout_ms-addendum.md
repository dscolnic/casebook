## The course at grade 6

Middle-school physical science, grade 6: circuits, energy and the grid

Roughly follow it; the gaps are useful information. If something important is
taught nowhere, say so in your prose summary.

1. A circuit is a loop, and a break anywhere stops it
2. Current is the flow, and voltage is the push behind it
3. Two ways to wire things: one after another, or side by side
4. Something in the way makes it harder for current to flow
5. Current through something makes heat, and that is why wires have limits
6. Energy used is power multiplied by time
7. A generator turns motion into electricity, and a magnet is how
8. Supply has to match demand, minute by minute
9. A graph of demand across a day, and why the peak matters
10. Some sources can be turned up on demand and some cannot
11. A safety device breaks the circuit before something is damaged
12. Something can be protected too eagerly, and cost you the supply
13. Measuring instruments need checking against a standard
14. Averages, and why one reading is not a measurement
15. Restarting a system that has stopped, in the right order
16. Deciding while the picture is incomplete

## The equations this course may compute

Substitution into a one-line formula, with numbers chosen to stay manageable.
The formula is there to show where the answer comes from, not to be an
arithmetic obstacle.

| Equation | What it is | Taught after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `energy = power × time` | how much electricity something uses | — |
| `how long = amount ÷ rate` | how long a store will last | `energy = power × time` |
| `part = whole × share` | a share of the demand, or of the customers | — |
| `average = total ÷ how many` | the mean of several readings | — |
| `spare = what can be made − what is being used` | how much room is left on the system | — |

**Nothing is asked before the equation it is built out of** — `equationOrder`
fails the game for it, and it counts a question that *computes* a number, not
one that mentions the relationship.

### What the senior-high course computes and this one does not

These are in the base game. None of them may be computed here. Where a stop
leans on one, that stop is a redesign, not a rewrite.

- `V = IR` — Ohm's law, on a conductor and on a whole network
- `P = IV, and P = I²R` — power delivered, and the loss that never arrives
- `V_rms = V_peak / √2` — why an AC quantity needs saying which one you mean
- `S = √(P² + Q²), pf = P/S` — apparent power and power factor
- `N₁/N₂ = V₁/V₂` — the transformer ratio, and why transmission is at high voltage
- `P = √3 · V_L · I_L · cos φ` — three-phase power from line quantities
- `df/dt = (P_gen − P_load) / 2H` — frequency as the running balance of supply and demand
- `ΔV ≈ I(R cos φ + X sin φ)` — voltage drop along a line, and why reactive power moves it
- `peak = base peak + sensitivity × degrees below` — how far cold moves an evening peak
- `E = Pt` — energy from power over time — the kilowatt-hour
- `η = E_out / E_in` — round-trip efficiency of storage
