## The course at grade 6

Middle-school Earth science and engineering design, grade 6

Roughly follow it; the gaps are useful information. If something important is
taught nowhere, say so in your prose summary.

1. Plates move, and a fault is the crack where they slip past each other
2. An earthquake sends waves through the ground, and some arrive first
3. How big the earthquake was, and how hard the shaking felt, are two different things
4. Distance from the fault changes what a place feels
5. The ground itself changes the shaking: soft ground shakes more
6. Wet sandy ground can behave like a liquid when it is shaken
7. Reading a map, a scale and a contour
8. Counting damage as a share, not as a number of buildings
9. Measure it more than once, and average the readings
10. A building carries its weight down a path, and the weakest needed link governs
11. Materials: what a crack means, and what a strength test can tell you
12. One sample is not the whole building
13. Aftershocks get rarer as time passes, and a chance is not a promise
14. Deciding with a cordon: how likely, and how bad, against what it costs people
15. Saying what you know and what you do not
16. An instrument that is wrong needs a check from something else

## The equations this course may compute

Substitution into a one-line formula, with numbers chosen to stay manageable.
The formula is there to show where the answer comes from, not to be an
arithmetic obstacle.

| Equation | What it is | Taught after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `speed = distance ÷ time` | how fast a wave travels through the ground | — |
| `distance = speed × time` | how far away the earthquake was | `speed = distance ÷ time` |
| `part = whole × share` | a share of the buildings, or of the people | — |
| `average = total ÷ how many` | the mean of repeated measurements | — |
| `how many times = bigger ÷ smaller` | comparing two shakings, or two strengths | — |
| `load on each metre = total weight ÷ length` | what a wall or a footing is carrying | — |

**Nothing is asked before the equation it is built out of** — `equationOrder`
fails the game for it, and it counts a question that *computes* a number, not
one that mentions the relationship.

### What the senior-high course computes and this one does not

These are in the base game. None of them may be computed here. Where a stop
leans on one, that stop is a redesign, not a rewrite.

- `d ≈ 8 km/s · (t_S − t_P)` — distance from the S-minus-P interval
- `M₀ = μ · A · D` — seismic moment, and what makes a big earthquake big
- `M_w = ⅔ log₁₀ M₀ − 6.06` — moment magnitude, and why the scale is logarithmic
- `T ≈ 0.1 N` — a building's natural period from its storeys
- `V = C_s · W` — base shear — the sideways force it is designed for
- `σ = F / A, ε = ΔL / L` — stress and strain, and the yield point between them
- `FoS = capacity / demand` — factor of safety as a ratio, not a feeling
- `σ' = σ − u` — effective stress, and how liquefaction happens
- `n(t) = K / (c + t)^p` — aftershock rate, falling as a power law
- `A_soft / A_rock` — site amplification — the same wave on two grounds
