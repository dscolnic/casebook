## The course at grade 6

Middle-school physical science, grade 6: matter, mixtures and measuring what is in water

Roughly follow it; the gaps are useful information. If something important is
taught nowhere, say so in your prose summary.

1. Everything is made of matter, and matter comes in solids, liquids and gases
2. A mixture is things sharing a space without becoming one another
3. Dissolving, and why some things dissolve and others do not
4. Concentration: how much of something is in how much of everything
5. Dilution: adding water lowers the concentration and not the amount
6. Separating a mixture, using a difference between the parts
7. Weight is conserved: what goes in has to come out somewhere
8. Measuring the same thing several times, and averaging
9. A blank and a standard: how a laboratory knows a number is real
10. A sample stands for a place and a moment, and only those
11. A limit is a number somebody chose, for a reason worth knowing
12. Acids and bases, and why the middle is where things are stable
13. Reactions give out heat or take it in, and heat speeds things up
14. Gases spread out, and the wind decides where
15. A treatment can move a problem rather than remove it
16. Saying what you know and how well you know it

## The equations this course may compute

Substitution into a one-line formula, with numbers chosen to stay manageable.
The formula is there to show where the answer comes from, not to be an
arithmetic obstacle.

| Equation | What it is | Taught after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `concentration = amount ÷ volume` | how much of something is in how much water | — |
| `amount = concentration × volume` | how much is really there | `concentration = amount ÷ volume` |
| `new concentration = old × old volume ÷ new volume` | what dilution does | — |
| `average = total ÷ how many` | the mean of several samples | — |
| `part = whole × share` | a percentage of a total | — |
| `depth lost each year = weight lost ÷ (how heavy it is × the area)` | how fast a wall is thinning | — |
| `how many times = bigger ÷ smaller` | comparing a reading with a limit | `concentration = amount ÷ volume` |

**Nothing is asked before the equation it is built out of** — `equationOrder`
fails the game for it, and it counts a question that *computes* a number, not
one that mentions the relationship.

### What the senior-high course computes and this one does not

These are in the base game. None of them may be computed here. Where a stop
leans on one, that stop is a redesign, not a rewrite.

- `n = m / M` — moles from a mass and a molar mass
- `PV = nRT` — the ideal gas law
- `c = n / V` — molarity as moles per unit volume
- `C₁V₁ = C₂V₂` — dilution, and carrying a dilution factor back
- `ΔTf = i·Kf·m` — how far dissolved salt drops a freezing point
- `n = It ÷ (zF)` — how much a current can deposit or remove
- `q = mcΔT` — calorimetry — heat from a temperature change
- `pH = −log[H⁺]` — acidity on a logarithmic scale
- `A = εlc` — Beer–Lambert, and the calibration curve it justifies
- `Kₛₚ = [Aᵃ][Bᵇ]` — the solubility product and the common-ion effect
- `percent yield = actual ÷ theoretical × 100` — yield against a stoichiometric prediction
- `rate = k[A]ⁿ` — a rate law, and the order it asserts
