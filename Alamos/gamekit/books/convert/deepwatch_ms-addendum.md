## The course at grade 6

Middle-school physical science, grade 6: sound and waves, floating and sinking, heat and circuits

Roughly follow it; the gaps are useful information. If something important is
taught nowhere, say so in your prose summary.

1. Sound is a wave, and it travels through water as well as air
2. An echo gives a distance, once you know the speed and the time
3. Loud and quiet, high and low: what a sound tells you about its source
4. Listening from two places to find where a sound came from
5. Hearing something quiet next to something loud
6. Density: why one thing floats and another sinks
7. Water pressure grows with depth
8. Adding weight changes what floats, and how fast it changes
9. Heat moves from hot to cold, and it needs a path
10. Circuits: a break, a short, and why water and electricity are dangerous
11. Air is a mixture, and the share of each gas matters
12. A rate: how fast something builds up or drains away
13. Reading a gauge, and taking a measurement more than once
14. Change one thing at a time, and watch what follows
15. Deciding under pressure: what can be undone, and what cannot
16. Two instruments that share a source are really one instrument

## The equations this course may compute

Substitution into a one-line formula, with numbers chosen to stay manageable.
The formula is there to show where the answer comes from, not to be an
arithmetic obstacle.

| Equation | What it is | Taught after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `speed = distance ÷ time` | how fast something is moving | — |
| `distance = speed × time ÷ 2` | range from an echo, out and back | `speed = distance ÷ time` |
| `amount = rate × time` | how much has built up, or drained away | `speed = distance ÷ time` |
| `density = weight ÷ volume` | what decides floating and sinking | — |
| `part = whole × share` | a percentage of a mixture | — |
| `average = total ÷ how many` | the mean of repeated readings | — |

**Nothing is asked before the equation it is built out of** — `equationOrder`
fails the game for it, and it counts a question that *computes* a number, not
one that mentions the relationship.

### What the senior-high course computes and this one does not

These are in the base game. None of them may be computed here. Where a stop
leans on one, that stop is a redesign, not a rewrite.

- `v = fλ` — wave speed, frequency and wavelength
- `Δf / f = v / c` — Doppler shift as a closing speed
- `dB = 10·log₁₀(P/P₀)` — the decibel, and why 3 dB is a doubling
- `SL − 2TL + TS − NL ≥ DT` — the sonar equation as a detection budget
- `sinθ₁ / v₁ = sinθ₂ / v₂` — Snell's law — why sound bends at a layer
- `d = ½vt` — echo ranging on a two-way travel time
- `p = ρgh` — hydrostatic pressure with depth
- `F_b = ρVg` — Archimedes — buoyancy from displaced volume
- `f_beat = |f₁ − f₂|` — beats between two close frequencies
- `PV = nRT` — the gas law behind a life-support margin
