## The course at grade 6

Middle-school life science, grade 6: germs, how they spread, and how a town answers

Roughly follow it; the gaps are useful information. If something important is
taught nowhere, say so in your prose summary.

1. Some illnesses are caused by living things too small to see
2. A germ has to get from one person into another somehow
3. Where and when people got ill is evidence about how
4. Counting cases as a rate, not just as a number
5. A graph of cases over time, and what its shape says
6. One person passing it to more than one is why it grows
7. The body has defences, and they take time to build
8. A test can be wrong in two different directions
9. Comparing a group who had something with a group who did not
10. Repeats and numbers: a handful of cases proves little
11. Breaking one step of the chain stops the spread
12. Germs change, and a change can beat a treatment
13. Animals and people share some illnesses
14. What a measurement can honestly say, and what it cannot
15. Telling people what to do, and why it has to be doable
16. Deciding while the answer is still unknown

## The equations this course may compute

Substitution into a one-line formula, with numbers chosen to stay manageable.
The formula is there to show where the answer comes from, not to be an
arithmetic obstacle.

| Equation | What it is | Taught after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `rate = cases ÷ people` | how common something is, rather than how many | — |
| `part = whole × share` | a percentage of a group | — |
| `average = total ÷ how many` | the mean of several counts | — |
| `how many times = bigger ÷ smaller` | comparing two groups | `rate = cases ÷ people` |
| `how many after n rounds = start × 2 each round` | why an outbreak grows so fast | — |

**Nothing is asked before the equation it is built out of** — `equationOrder`
fails the game for it, and it counts a question that *computes* a number, not
one that mentions the relationship.

### What the senior-high course computes and this one does not

These are in the base game. None of them may be computed here. Where a stop
leans on one, that stop is a redesign, not a rewrite.

- `Rₑ = R₀ × S` — effective reproduction number against a susceptible fraction
- `N(t) = N₀ · 2^(t/T_d)` — exponential growth from a doubling time
- `sensitivity = TP / (TP+FN)` — and specificity = TN / (TN+FP) — what a test misses
- `PPV = (prev·sens) / (prev·sens + (1−prev)(1−spec))` — why a good test fails at low prevalence
- `risk = events / people at risk` — and NNT = 1 / (control risk − treated risk)
- `CFR = deaths / cases` — case fatality, and what its denominator hides
- `pH = pKa + log([A⁻]/[HA])` — Henderson–Hasselbalch — buffering in a body
