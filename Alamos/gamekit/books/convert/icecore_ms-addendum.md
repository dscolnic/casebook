## The course at grade 6

Middle-school Earth and environmental science, grade 6: layers, records and climate

Roughly follow it; the gaps are useful information. If something important is
taught nowhere, say so in your prose summary.

1. Snow that never melts piles up in layers, one year at a time
2. Counting layers is counting years, and deeper means older
3. Deeper layers are squashed thinner, so depth is not the same as age
4. A marker found in many places lets separate records be lined up
5. Something measured today can stand in for something nobody measured then
6. A ratio can be turned into a temperature using a known relationship
7. Air trapped in bubbles is a sample of the sky from long ago
8. The trapped air is younger than the ice around it
9. Reading a graph: what the axes say, and what the wiggles mean
10. Averages, and why one year proves nothing on its own
11. Rates: how fast something builds up, and how long a job will take
12. Two records that agree are stronger than one that is longer
13. Contamination: a sample that has been got at tells you about the getting
14. What a measurement can honestly say, and what it cannot
15. Keeping a record somebody else can use
16. Cold, power and daylight are the limits a polar camp works inside

The formula is there to show where the answer comes from, not to be an

| `average = total ÷ how many` | the mean of several years, or several samples | — |

### What the senior-high course computes and this one does not

These are in the base game. None of them may be computed here. Where a stop
leans on one, that stop is a redesign, not a rewrite.

- `δ¹⁸O = (R_sample / R_standard − 1) × 1000 ‰` — an isotope ratio, written as a deviation from a standard
- `ΔT = Δδ¹⁸O / α,  α ≈ 0.67 ‰ per °C` — the isotope thermometer, and the slope it rests on
- `b = λ × ρ_snow / ρ_ice` — accumulation, converted to ice equivalent
- `λ(z) = λ₀ (H − z) / H` — layer thinning with depth, as ice flows outwards
- `Δage ≈ z_close × ρ̄ / b` — why the gas in a bubble is younger than the ice around it
- `age(z) = Σ Δz / λ(z)` — building a depth–age scale by adding up layers
- `N(t) = N₀ e^(−t/τ)` — radiometric dating, where nothing can be counted
- `ΔF = 5.35 ln(C / C₀)  W m⁻²` — turning a gas concentration into an energy imbalance
- `a feature survives only if its thickness > 2σ` — diffusion sets the finest thing a core can still show
- `SE = s / √n` — how much of an apparent trend is just noise
