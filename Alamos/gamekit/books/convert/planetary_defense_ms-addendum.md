## The course at grade 6

Middle-school astronomy and Earth & space science, grade 6

Roughly follow it; the gaps are useful information. If something important is
taught nowhere, say so in your prose summary.

1. Telling a real object from an artefact, by repeating the observation
2. Position, time and speed: how fast something moves across the sky
3. Repeated measurements and their average
4. Uncertainty as a range, not a mistake
5. Scale and distance in the solar system
6. Ratios and unit rates in a measurement
7. Brightness and distance: the same object is fainter further away
8. Size and albedo: two explanations for one brightness
9. Choosing the measurement that tells two explanations apart
10. Colour and light as evidence of what something is made of
11. Radar: distance from an echo, using speed and time
12. Rotation, and a pattern that repeats in a measurement
13. Impact energy: mass and speed, and why speed matters more
14. Changing motion with a push, and why an early push does more
15. Deciding what to tell people, and when
16. Independent checks: a second method that could disagree

## The equations this course may compute

Substitution into a one-line formula, with numbers chosen to stay manageable.
The formula is there to show where the answer comes from, not to be an
arithmetic obstacle.

| Equation | What it is | Taught after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `speed = distance ÷ time` | how fast something is moving | — |
| `distance = speed × time` | distance from a radar echo | `speed = distance ÷ time` |
| `average = total ÷ how many` | the mean of repeated measurements | — |
| `KE = ½ × m × v²` | the energy an impact carries | `speed = distance ÷ time` |
| `change in position = change in speed × time` | why a deflection works better early | `speed = distance ÷ time` |

**Nothing is asked before the equation it is built out of** — `equationOrder`
fails the game for it, and it counts a question that *computes* a number, not
one that mentions the relationship.

### What the senior-high course computes and this one does not

These are in the base game. None of them may be computed here. Where a stop
leans on one, that stop is a redesign, not a rewrite.

- `θ = s / d` — the small-angle formula — angular size to physical size
- `d = baseline / parallax angle` — distance from a parallax shift
- `F = L / 4πd²` — inverse-square brightness, and the size–albedo degeneracy
- `KE = ½mv²` — impact energy, and why speed dominates it
- `p = mv` — momentum, and momentum transfer in a deflection
- `v² = v∞² + v_esc²` — gravitational focusing of impact speed
- `v = √(GM / r)` — orbital speed, and the energy that goes with it
- `Δλ / λ = v / c` — Doppler shift as a line-of-sight speed
- `E[X] = Σ p·x` — expected value — a consequence weighted by its probability
- `λ_max T = b` — Wien's law — a temperature read off a thermal spectrum
