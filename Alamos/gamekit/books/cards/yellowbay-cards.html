1.2 The one number that names an element Metrology Bay · a person · CHOICE

Ask9w

Which difference between two atoms makes them different elements?

Takes as read

an atom is a nucleus of protons and neutrons with electrons around it

Verdict86w

The nuclear charge is what an element is. Change the neutrons and you have another isotope of the same element, with the same chemistry and a different mass. Ask11w

How much energy does one photon of the exposure light carry?

Takes as read

light of a given wavelength arrives in packets of a fixed energy · a nanometre is a thousand-millionth of a metre

Verdict91w

Energy per photon is set by wavelength alone, and the resist responds to single photons rather than to the total energy landing on it. The exposure photon carries about eight times ten to the minus nineteen joules. The amber one, at more than twice the wavelength, carries less than half of that. So it falls under the threshold the resist chemistry needs. That is why the brightness of the safelight is close to irrelevant and its colour is not. More photons that individually cannot do the job still cannot do it.

Takeaway28w

The energy of a photon rises as its wavelength falls, so the wavelength decides what light can do and the brightness only decides how much of it arrives.

2.2 What a gas is doing while nothing is happening Deposition Bay · a person · SEQUENCE

Scene39w

Guide48w

At one temperature every molecule in the chamber has the same average energy, and energy is shared between mass and speed. A heavy molecule therefore carries that energy as mass and a light one as speed. Rank them by the mass on the label and the order follows.

Ask10w

Order the four gases in the chamber by average speed.

Takes as read

temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the particles · a molar mass can be read off a chemical formula

Verdict91w

Temperature fixes the average kinetic energy, which is a half of the mass times the speed squared, so at one temperature a heavier molecule must be slower. Argon is twenty times the mass of hydrogen and moves about four and a half times more slowly. The speed goes as the square root of the mass rather than as the mass. That square root is why the spread in speeds is far narrower than the spread in masses. It is also why hydrogen reaches a leak first without being twenty times quicker.

Takeaway18w

At one temperature all molecules carry the same average energy, so the light ones must be moving fastest.

2.3 How tightly the nucleus holds on Metrology Bay · a room · BALLPARK

Scene42w

Aaron Dorsey, the metrology technician, has a table on the bench. It gives the energy needed to knock an electron out of each element on the wafer. Ferreira wants to know why that energy is so much larger for some of them.

Guide51w

The attraction between two charges depends on both charges and on the distance twice over. Two of the tiles describe the distance. One is the distance itself and one is its square, and only one of those belongs underneath the line. Choosing wrongly between them is the whole of the arithmetic.

Ask10w

How strongly is silicon's outer electron attracted, in relative units?

Takes as read

a nucleus carries one positive charge per proton · an electron carries one negative charge

Verdict89w

Attraction between charges is the product of the charges divided by the square of the distance. So moving an electron twice as far out weakens the hold by a factor of four rather than two. The number that comes out here is an overestimate, and deliberately so. It uses all fourteen protons, and an outer electron in a real silicon atom does not feel all fourteen. What sits between it and the nucleus is the subject of the next stop, and this arithmetic is what it has to correct.

Takeaway18w

Attraction rises with the charges and falls with the square of the separation, so distance costs twice over.

End of the day19w

3.1 The charge that is left over Metrology Bay · a room · BALLPARK

Scene34w

Ferreira has a phosphorus atom on the board with its fifteen protons written up and ten of its electrons circled. Dorsey's measured ionisation energy is a fifth of what the full nuclear charge predicts.

Guide57w

An outer electron is pulled by the nucleus and pushed by every electron between it and the nucleus, and the two nearly cancel. What is left over is a much smaller charge than the proton count. The tiles hold the protons, the inner electrons and the outer ones, and only two of the three enter the subtraction.

Ask9w

What effective nuclear charge does phosphorus's outer electron feel?

Takes as read

electrons in filled inner shells lie between the nucleus and the outer electrons · like charges repel

Verdict92w

Ten electrons in the filled inner shells sit between the nucleus and the outer shell. They cancel ten of the fifteen positive charges, so an outer electron responds to about five rather than fifteen. Electrons in the same shell are at roughly the same distance, and they shield each other hardly at all. That is why the outer five are left out of the subtraction. This is also why effective charge rises across a row while the shell stays the same: protons are being added and the shielding is not keeping up.

Takeaway22w

Effective nuclear charge is the proton count minus the electrons closer in, and it is what an outer electron actually responds to.

Why agreement is weak evidence.
