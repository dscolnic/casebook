1.1 Why the vault is cold Conservation & Viability · Seed Vault · a room · BALLPARK

Scene34w

Mbaye has two lots of the same accession on the bench. 1 spent 4 years at minus 18 degrees and 6% moisture. The other sat in a warm shed and now germinates at 31%.

Guide62w

Deterioration is a chemical rate, so cold and dryness each multiply the time a seed lasts, and the two multiply together. The shed lot is the baseline. Work out how much longer the vault lot should last, using the temperature factor and the moisture factor and nothing else — two of the tiles are quantities from a different part of the problem.

Ask15w

How many times longer should the vault lot last than the lot in the shed?

Behind the button136w

Why a rate rather than a switch. A dry, cold seed is not dormant and not protected; it is still repairing damage and still accumulating it, only slowly. That is why storage life is measured in half-lives of viability and why a seed lot has no shelf date, only a rate.

What the two factors do separately. Around this range each 5 °C drop roughly doubles storage life, and each 2 percentage points of seed moisture removed does much the same. Neither kills anything and neither stops anything — both slow the same reactions.

Why they multiply. The reactions are the same reactions, and each factor divides their rate, so the effects compound rather than add. That is what makes a cold dry vault worth its electricity: sixty-four times is a different institution from eight times.

Takes as read

orthodox seeds are seeds that can survive drying and cold storage · seed viability as a rate: what a stored seed is still doing — taken as read

Verdict128w

Cold, dry storage works on a rate. A stored seed is still repairing damage and still taking it on. Both variables slow the same reactions. The shed sat at about 2 °C, with seed at 14 per cent moisture. The vault holds minus eighteen and 6 per cent. That is 20 °C colder. At roughly a doubling per 5 °C, it is 2⁴, or 16 times. It is also 8 points drier. Halve the moisture again and the same thing happens, so that is another few doublings. Both factors divide the rate of one process, so they multiply rather than add. The answer is about 64 times, not 32. That gap is the difference between holding an accession for a working lifetime and holding it for a career.

Takeaway14w

Cold and low moisture extend orthodox-seed life by slowing the reactions that drive deterioration.

2.1 Counting a generation Heredity & Crossing · Crossing Hall · a room · BALLPARK

Scene30w

400 F2 plants, and 306 of them show the resistant reaction while 94 do not. Bengtsson asks what that number is supposed to be, and whether it is close enough.

Guide66w

Work out what a single dominant locus predicts, then compare. Two heterozygous parents give three resistant to one susceptible, so the expected count is the number of plants times three quarters. The two-gene alternative, where either dominant allele is enough, predicts fifteen to one instead. Those two expectations are far apart at four hundred plants, which is what makes the observed count evidence rather than decoration.

Ask19w

How many of the 400 F2 plants should show the resistant reaction if a single dominant locus governs it?

Behind the button166w

Why the tiles carry labels. A bare number cannot be checked against the relationship it is going into. Reading the label is how a quantity belonging to a different part of the problem is caught before it is placed — and two of the tiles here belong to the alternative model, not to this one.

Where the three quarters comes from. Each parent carries one resistant and one susceptible allele, and meiosis puts one of the two into each gamete. Four equally likely combinations follow, and three of them contain at least one resistant allele. Dominance is what makes those three look alike.

Why the comparison needs both models. A count near three hundred is only evidence for one locus because a rival model predicts something else. Fifteen to one puts twenty five susceptible plants in four hundred rather than a hundred, so a single tray of this size separates them. A ratio quoted without the alternative it beats is a number nobody can argue with.

Takes as read

a plant carries two copies of each gene and passes on one

Verdict105w

F1 plants carry one resistance allele and one susceptible allele. Meiosis separates those alleles, so 2 heterozygous parents give a 3 : 1 phenotype expectation when resistance is dominant. In 400 offspring that is 400 × 3 ÷ 4 = 300 resistant and 100 susceptible. The observed 306 and 94 sit close to it. 2 independently assorting genes where either dominant allele gives resistance predict 15 : 1, which is 400 × 15 ÷ 16 = 375 resistant and 25 susceptible. The counts therefore support one dominant locus and rule the two-locus alternative out, which no single plant in the tray could have told you.

Takeaway15w

The proportions in a whole generation carry information that no single plant in it does.

2.2 One disagreement, two possible causes Markers & Gene Expression · Molecular Laboratory · a person · DEGENERACY

Scene40w

Nakagawa has 400 F2 plants scored by a linked PCR marker and by resistance. 11 carry the marker but fail the resistance test. The PCR controls and gel bands look clean; the marker lies about four centimorgans from the gene.

Guide53w

Two controls slide recombination rate and test error along a family of combinations that all produce eleven disagreements in four hundred plants. See how wide that family is before naming a cause. Then bring in the direct-gene test, which fails for one of those explanations and not the other, and watch it collapse.

Ask16w

Which combinations can explain the first disagreement rate, and which one survives the direct-gene PCR check?

Behind the button142w

Why one disagreement rate cannot separate the two. A marker four centimorgans from the gene is expected to be separated from it in about four plants per hundred by recombination. A test that occasionally misreads produces the same disagreements. Eleven in four hundred is consistent with several mixtures of the two.

What the direct-gene test does. It asks about the gene itself rather than about the marker, so recombination between them is irrelevant to it. Plants that carry the gene and failed the resistance test are test error; plants that lack the gene are recombinants.

Why the clean controls are not an answer. Controls show the reaction worked on known material. They cannot detect a plant-specific failure — poor template, an inhibitor, a mislabelled well — which is exactly the kind of error that produces a handful of disagreements in four hundred.

Takes as read

PCR uses primers to amplify a chosen DNA region · gel electrophoresis separates DNA fragments by size · a nearby marker can be separated from a gene by crossing over · independent assortment, and the ratios two genes give instead — taken as read

Verdict77w

PCR copies a chosen DNA region through repeated denaturation, primer annealing, and extension. Gel electrophoresis can then separate the resulting DNA fragments by size. Here that tool reads a marker near the resistance gene, not the gene itself. Marker–trait disagreement can therefore come from recombination or assay error. One disagreement rate cannot separate those causes. A second PCR assay aimed inside the resistance gene adds independent evidence. When it confirms the gene call, linkage and recombination remain.

Takeaway19w

PCR can identify a DNA sequence, but a linked DNA marker can still separate from the gene it predicts.

3.1 Frequencies, not plants Population Genetics · Genetic Resources Office · a room · BALLPARK

Behind the button106w

Why an estimate rather than a calculation. Every quantity on this board is already measured or stated, so nothing here has to be derived. Takes as read

Verdict72w

3.3 Move the line, not the story Field Trials & Selection · Field Laboratory · a room · CONTROL

Scene38w

Quiroga has three microplot positions across the slope and enough seed from two numbered lines for a quick repeat. The trainee’s strip layout put each line in only one place. Yield scatter from repeated microplots is already known.

Guide68w

The number you are watching is plot yield, and repeated microplots already tell you how much it scatters on its own. Three things about the setup can be changed: which line is sown, where on the slope it sits, and how thickly. A change counts only if it moves yield by more than that scatter. Change one, run the microplot, restore it, and name the variable yield follows.

Ask17w

Which variable moves yield by more than the repeat scatter and then returns when you restore it?

Behind the button166w

Why the trainee's layout cannot answer the question. Each line was sown in one place, so line and position changed together. If the numbers differ, nothing in the data can say whether the better line or the better ground did it. That is what a confound is, and it is not fixed by more careful measurement of the same plots.

What the repeat scatter is for. Sow the same line twice in the same conditions and the two plots do not match: soil varies, weather varies, a hare gets in. That spread is the yardstick. A difference smaller than it is a measurement of the field rather than of the treatment.

Why position is the one to suspect on a slope. Water, depth and warmth all change down a slope, and all three move yield. A field trial is laid out to break the link between treatment and position on purpose — blocks, randomisation, replication — and this stop is what happens when that was skipped.

Takes as read

field position can change crop yield · a causal comparison changes one candidate cause while holding the others fixed · dominance, and why one allele is enough — taken as read

Verdict81w

A strip design changes genotype and ground together, so either can explain the yield difference. The control run holds the line and sowing density fixed while moving only the plot position. That change shifts yield far beyond the repeat scatter. Restoring the original position restores the reading. Changing the line on the same ground produces only a small shift in this authored test. The result shows why blocks and randomisation are needed before genotype can be credited for a field difference.

Takeaway23w

When genotype and field position always change together, their effects are confounded; a control test separates them by changing one at a time.

4.2 What the trial can actually claim Field Trials & Selection · Field Laboratory · a person · STRESS

Scene31w

Four seasons, one site, four replicates each. WM-712 is ahead by 11% in the 2 dry years and by 2% in the wet ones. The merchant's leaflet says "11% higher yielding".

Guide74w

The claim on the leaflet rests on an assumption about how dry next season is, and the slider is that assumption — the soil-moisture deficit, where a low number is a wet year. Three candidate sentences are scored against two criteria. Take the slider down to a wet season and see which sentence is still true, because the one that reads best at the expected deficit is not the one that survives being wrong.

Ask17w

Which sentence about WM-712 is still true if next season's rainfall lands anywhere in the plausible range?

Behind the button167w

What a trial measures. Four replicates at one site over four seasons separate a line from the pieces of ground it was grown on, and they do it under the weather those four seasons happened to bring. The number and its conditions are one object; quoting the number alone makes a different claim.

Why the expected season is the trap. At the four-season average deficit the bare eleven per cent needs the fewest words of qualification, which is exactly what a leaflet optimises for. Take the deficit down to a wet year and the sentence is wrong by a factor of five, and a buyer who paid for eleven and got two has been misled by a figure nobody fabricated.

What survives. A sentence that names the condition it was measured under stays true whatever next season does, and costs the merchant eleven more words on the leaflet. That is the trade being made, and genotype-by-environment interaction is the name for the reason it cannot be avoided.

Takes as read

a trial measures lines under the conditions the trial happened in

Verdict142w

The eleven per cent is real and it belongs to two dry seasons. Set next season wet and the same line yields two per cent more than the standard, so a buyer who planned on eleven has been misled by a figure nobody fabricated. The conditional sentence gives up the headline and stays true across the whole rainfall range, which is what robustness means here: not the claim that scores best at the expected value, but the one that is still correct at the edges. The regional claim is worse than both — one site cannot support it at any rainfall, because nothing in the trial varied the region. Genotype-by-environment interaction is exactly this: a line's advantage is a property of the line and the conditions together, and a number quoted without its conditions is a different claim wearing the same digits.

Takeaway18w

Behind the button156w

Why a distance is the useful form. Verdict152w
