# The briefing pass — a two-sentence stake, and a reason per call

How to cut a campaign's daily opening card down to two sentences, and replace the
boilerplate under each objective with a line saying why that call is on **today's**
list. Worked first on `redsand` (Red Sand, AP Chemistry, 15 sols, 56 stops); the
numbers at the bottom are that run.

It is a copy pass. No question changes, no lesson moves, no world work. Two book
fields and one line in the manifest — and that one line also drops a third block of
boilerplate off the card, below.

**This is one of a pair.** `PLACEMENT_PASS.md` is the other: where each question is
*asked*. They are independent and can be done in either order, with one join between
them — placement moves which call is a person, and a reason line that opens *"She
wants…"* on a call that is now a room is left pointing at nobody. Whichever you do
second, re-read the objective lines afterwards. Both were worked on `redsand`, and
both carry a per-campaign procedure.

---

## What is wrong with the card this fixes

Two separate defects, both on the plan card, both invisible to every gate until
you read the card as a player who has already read fourteen of them.

**1. The stake is a briefing, not a stake.** The shipped shape is a hundred-odd
words in a dozen sentences: the tally, the arithmetic, who wants what, who owns the
specification, what they each say, what the player does, and what it costs if the
fortnight slips. All of it true, most of it a second telling. Red Sand's fifteen
averaged **126 words in 11.6 sentences**, none shorter than 119, and sol 1 spent
three of its twelve sentences introducing two people by name and job title before
it reached what the player does. The player reads that once with attention and then skims it
fourteen times, which means the one sentence that changes each morning is buried in
the one paragraph that does not.

**2. Every objective's second line is the area's job description.** Under `Go to
Reactor Hall` the card prints *"How much methane a pass can give, and what moves
that limit"* — the group's `desc`, generated by `reasonFor` in
`engine/core/app.js`. It is true, it is well written, and it is **the same sentence
every sol the day sends you there**. It says what the room is for. It never says
why you are walking there this morning, which is the only question the player
actually has while looking at a list of three places.

The fix for both is the same idea: the card should carry what is true *today* and
nothing that was true yesterday too.

## The shape

**The stake — a date stamp and two sentences.**

```
Sol 293. The cold end stopped passing liquid three hours after the hot run began.
Today you find where the water went, and follow it back to the ground it came from.
```

- Sentence one: the single most important thing that is happening. One fact with a
  number in it beats three without.
- Sentence two: what the player does about it, opening `Today you …`.
- **No names.** Thirteen people and forty words is a quarter of the card spent
  introducing somebody the player has not met. They are met on the calls, in the
  scenes and in the debrief, which is where a person belongs.
- The date stamp (`Sol 293.`) is its own sentence and is doing real work: it is
  what makes the card read as a morning rather than a chapter, and it pulls the
  words-per-sentence average down, which is most of the reading score.

**The reason — the motivation for this call, in one sentence.**

```yaml
- group: KINET
  task: Say what 40 degrees actually buys
  title: Rate is not yield
  reason: >-
    She wants the reactor run hotter tonight, and whether that would raise the
    output at all is today's question
```

The question it answers is **"why am I walking here, and what has it got to do with
what the stake just told me I am doing today?"** Not what the area is for, and — the
mistake worth naming — **not a fact about the science**. Two drafts of Red Sand got
written before this landed, and both failed in an instructive way:

| draft | the line under `Talk to Ingrid Sundqvist` | what is wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| generated | *How fast the reaction goes, what the catalyst does, and what stops it working* | the area's job description. True every sol, so it says nothing about this one |
| first | *The 40-degree request lives or dies on whether a faster bed is also a fuller one* | **dangling**: the 40-degree request was in the old long stake, and the pass deleted it |
| second | *Speeding a reaction up and getting more out of it are two different wins* | a science fact. Accessible, self-contained — and no reason to walk anywhere |
| shipped | *She wants the reactor run hotter tonight, and whether that would raise the output at all is today's question* | supplies its own context, names the stake, poses the question |

So, in order:

- **Tie it to the day's stated job.** The stake ends with `Today you …`; every reason
  should visibly serve that clause. Sol 1's is *find what holds the output down*, and
  its three calls are the place methane is made, the person who wants it made faster,
  and the place it is lost — each said in those terms.
- **Carry its own context.** Assume the player has read the two-sentence stake and
  nothing else. Any incident the reason leans on, it has to supply itself.
- **Give the pressure, not the physics.** Something is waiting, missing, disputed, or
  about to be signed. `alamos-curriculum` owns the teaching; this line owns the
  motive.
- **Pose the question, never answer it.** *"whether that would raise the output at
  all is today's question"* sets up the stop; *"a faster bed is not a fuller one"*
  would have answered it on the plan card.
- **Person stops: use the pronoun, not the name.** The call above it already reads
  `Talk to Ingrid Sundqvist`, so the reason opens *"She wants …"*. Check who the stop
  actually resolves to before writing — `shapeMissions` marks the person stop and
  `getPersonIdForStop` picks `list[(week - 1 + earlier) % list.length]` from that
  area's roster, which is often **not** the person named in the lesson's scene.
- **12–21 words, one sentence, no trailing full stop.** The generated fallback strips
  a period, so an authored one is visibly a different shape. Do not restate the task —
  it is printed directly above.

**The delivery line comes off the card too.** `stakeStyle: 'brief'` also suppresses
`deliveryPlanLine` — the sentence under the stake reading *"Today you add the feed
limit table to The Propellant Handover. 0 of 15 are on the board in Plant Control."*
Read on its own it is a good line. Read in place it is the third block of prose above
the objectives, on a card whose whole point is now that the player reaches the
objectives — and it is the same sentence with one noun and one number changed, every
morning of the campaign.

Nothing is lost by dropping it. `checkDelivery` already requires the opening card to
name what the campaign is building, so the player is told once, properly; and
`deliveryGainHTML` prints the running count on the card that **closes** each day,
which is where *"9 of 15"* actually means something — it is a thing you just earned
rather than a thing you have not done yet. The plan card was the one of the three
places that had to announce it before the fact.

**Do this for every campaign that takes the pass**, and consider it for the rest: the
line is on the plan card of all thirty-five today, and the argument against it does
not depend on the stake being brief. It is bundled into `stakeStyle` rather than
given a flag of its own because it is the same edit — a morning card that stops
explaining itself and starts pointing at the day.

## The gates, and the two you have to move

`npm run check <theme>` has three that care about this card.

**`engine/dev/checkStory.mjs` — the stake.** Its rules were written for the long
form and two of them fail a brief one outright, so the style is opted into on the
manifest:

```js
// themes/<theme>/theme.js
stakeStyle: 'brief',
```

With that set, three things change and nothing else does.

**The floors go — both of them.** The stake's 90-word floor becomes none, and so does
the opening card's 70. This is the part worth arguing about, so here is the argument:
a floor was written against a card that had to carry a fortnight of context, and the
whole claim of the brief style is that it does not. Every floor can only ever say
*"say it at greater length"*, which is the thing being removed. What a card must
contain is checked directly — the `Today you …` clause, the time cue, the authority
clause, a closing line with a number in it — and those beats are the real gate. A
word count on top of them is an instruction to pad. **The ceilings stay**, because
length is the failure being fixed: 70 words for a stake, 180 for the opening.

**The roster rule drops to a note.** A brief stake has no room for a name and is not
meant to carry one; the cast is met on the calls instead.

Nothing else is relaxed. A brief stake still has to say when it is happening in its
first two sentences, still has to contain a `Today you …` clause, still may not
contain any day's answer verbatim, and is still read against `audience.grade + 2` —
and `plainCards` still holds every card to grade 6.5 whatever the theme declares.

The time cue is the one that catches people out. `checkStory`'s `TIME` list is a
literal alternation — `hour(s)`, `day(s)`, `week(s)`, `month(s)`, `year(s)`,
`minute(s)`, `watch`, `shift`, `stage`, `phase`, `overnight`, `tonight`,
`last night`, `this morning`, `since`, `ago`, `before`, `after`, `at dawn`,
`by sunrise`, the ordinals, the months, the weekdays and a four-digit year. **A
campaign's own day noun is not on it** — `sol`/`sols` matches nothing, and neither
does a bare date stamp. Two sentences give you two chances to land a cue and the
date stamp burns one of them, so in practice it has to sit in sentence one.

**`engine/dev/plainCards.mjs` — the reading level.** This is the gate that will
actually stop you, and it is a **ratchet**: a campaign may not gain a card over
grade 6.5, and its worst card may not get worse. This is the counter-intuitive half
of the pass. **The long cards were already passing** — Red Sand's fifteen came in at
a mean of grade 4.7 with nothing over the bar, because a dozen short declarative
sentences is exactly what Flesch-Kincaid rewards. Condensing is compounding: the
same facts in two sentences means subordinate clauses, and the first draft of the
fifteen took the campaign's worst card from **5.8 to 11.0**, put eleven cards over
the bar and twelve sentences over the 28-word cap — while every other check in the
repo stayed green.

The arithmetic is why. Flesch-Kincaid is `0.39·(words/sentence) + 11.8·(syllables/word)
− 15.59`. A chemistry campaign is stuck near 1.5 syllables a word — *methane*,
*catalyst*, *electrolysis* are the course and you are not allowed to trade them
away — so **`words/sentence` is the only handle you have**. Two 25-word sentences
put you around grade 9 on vocabulary you cannot change; two sentences of 15–18
words plus a two-word date stamp lands at grade 3–5 with the same nouns in it.
Score before you paste: fifteen cards took three iterations, not one.

**`engine/dev/checkNames.mjs` — the names you just deleted.** The long stake was
introducing the cast, and `introRule` did not care where the introduction happened,
only that it came before the first bare surname. Take the names off fifteen cards
and seven people are suddenly first named in a scene with no job beside them.

This is the one thing in the pass that reaches outside the two fields, and it is
not optional — it is the check failing, not a note. The fix is mechanical and
`checkNames` prints the exact scene and the exact job: put the job beside the name
at that first mention.

```
- Herrera will not accept 'run it hotter' as a plan.
+ Tomás Herrera, the reactor and yield lead, will not accept 'run it hotter' as a plan.
```

Watch the scene's word budget while you do it — a scene is 30–45 words of situation
and a job title costs four or five of them, so something else usually has to go.

## While you are here: is the delivery the right thing?

Not strictly part of the pass, and it came out of it anyway — because the opening
card is where a wrong delivery shows.

Every course campaign builds one named thing over its fortnight, `theme.delivery`,
one piece a day on a board in a room. Red Sand's was **The Propellant Handover**: a
document, fifteen findings, written up for the next crew. It survived every gate for
the life of the game and it was the wrong object.

The tell was on the opening card. A draft of the shortened card named it and stopped
— *"The next crew gets your propellant handover"* — and the first person to read it
asked what a propellant handover is. The fix looked like a gloss, and eight words of
colon did the job. But the real finding is the one underneath:

> **A delivery that needs to be explained on the opening card is usually the wrong
> delivery.** The thing the fortnight builds should be the thing the fiction is
> already about. If you have to define it, the campaign is building a description of
> its stakes instead of its stakes.

Red Sand is about filling a rocket. The delivery is now **A Full Tank** — six point
six tonnes of methane and twenty-three of oxygen, in time for the window — and the
fifteen pieces are the reasons there is more in it than there would have been:
*The cold line passing liquid again*, *The poison found and cut off*, *The tank full,
eleven sols early*. Three things fell out, all of them gains:

- **The opening card needs no gloss.** A full tank explains itself, and the card is
  68 words rather than the 82 the gloss cost.
- **The board moved to where the thing is.** `delivery.where` went from Plant Control
  to the Cold End, where the liquid actually goes into the tanks, so the piece a day
  earns is read standing next to what it went into.
- **The ending card stopped being the odd one out.** It already closed on *"The
  rocket lifts full. You are the one who filled it."* The delivery was the last
  surface still counting pages.

**Check this on every campaign.** The question is one line: *is what this game builds
the thing at stake, or a document about the thing at stake?* A report, a file, a
handover, a case, a dossier — a noun on that list is not proof of the fault, but it
is where to look first. The paperwork framing is the easy one to author, which is
why it is the one that keeps happening. What it costs is the whole dramatic point of
a fortnight's work.

Mechanically it is a small change: `name`, `what`, `where` and fifteen `pieces` in
`theme.js`, then re-read the opening and ending cards for anything that named the old
one. `checkDelivery` holds the shape — pieces unique, none identical to its day's
takeaway, `what` between 12 and 48 words, the opening card naming every distinctive
word of the name, and `where` an area with somewhere to stand.

## The HUD strip: five readings down to three

Same pass, one surface up. The status strip stood over the world at all times and
carried **Time left · Calls · Mission · Projection · Funds**. Three of those five were
not things the player acts on.

| reading | verdict |
| --- | --- |
| Time left today | **kept**, and relabelled — it is the day's clock, not the campaign's, and "Time left" did not say so |
| Calls — *2 of 3* | **gone**. `#objective`, nine pixels below, already names every open call in full: *"Still open: Go to Reactor Hall · Talk to Ingrid Sundqvist"*. The box counted a list the player can read whole |
| Mission — *4 of 15* | **replaced by the delivery meter**, below |
| Projection — *Great / Good / Bad* | **gone**. A verdict on the campaign, permanently over the world. It changes nothing you do at half past two on sol 4 — it only tells you how you are being marked. Still computed, still on the command board for a player who wants to ask |
| Funds | **kept** |

**The meter.** *"Mission 4 of 15"* counted the game — the same fact a chapter number
gives, carrying none of the reason anybody is playing. The repo had already decided
what a fortnight is for: `theme.delivery` is the one named thing a campaign builds, a
piece a day. So the box became that arithmetic said as the thing at stake filling up:
a label and a bar, no figure.

- **No number beside it, deliberately.** The bar *is* the reading. *"4 of 15"* next to
  it is the chapter number coming back in through the side door, and the exact count
  is on the board in the room where the pieces are kept.
- **It counts pieces earned, not days elapsed.** A day whose calls went badly still
  advances `state.week` and does not fill the tank, so the bar can lag the calendar —
  which is a reading the old box could not give at any width.

**Every game gets this for free; each one needs one line.** The engine drives the meter
off `theme.delivery`, so a campaign only supplies the label:

```js
delivery: {
  name: 'A Full Tank',
  meter: 'Fuel needed',      // two or three words, above the bar
  …
}
```

`meter` names the **goal**, not the progress — the bar says how far along, the label
says what is filling. Without it the delivery's `name` is used, which is usually too
long for a 88px box, so write one. A campaign with **no** `delivery` at all — the ten
Quick Discoveries, which build nothing over a fortnight — keeps *"Mission N of M"*, and
should: a meter of nothing is worse than a chapter number.

## Procedure

```sh
cd gamekit
```

1. **Dump what is there.** Read every stake and every stop side by side; you are
   writing forty words from a hundred and you need the day's stops in front of you
   to know which fact is the load-bearing one.

   ```sh
   node --input-type=module -e "
   import fs from 'fs';
   import { parseYaml } from './tools/yaml-lite.mjs';
   const b = parseYaml(fs.readFileSync('books/<theme>.yml','utf8'));
   b.missions.forEach((m,i) => {
     console.log('=== ' + (i+1) + ' ' + m.title);
     console.log('STAKE: ' + String(m.stake).replace(/\n/g,' '));
     (m.stops||[]).forEach(s => console.log('  [' + s.group + '] ' + s.title + ' | ' + (s.task||'')));
   });"
   ```

2. **Draft the stakes and score them before they go in the book.** The scorer is
   twenty lines and it saves the whole loop:

   ```js
   import { fleschKincaid } from './tools/readability.js';
   const words = s => s.trim().split(/\s+/).filter(Boolean);
   const sents = s => s.split(/(?<=[.!?])\s+/).filter(x => x.trim());
   // fail if: grade > 6.5, any sentence > 28 words, total < 30 or > 70 words,
   //          no TIME cue in the first two sentences, no /Today\b[^.]{0,80}\byou\b/,
   //          any roster surname appears.
   ```

   Iterate until every card is clean. Do not put a card in the book to find out.

3. **Write one `reason:` per authored stop.** Every stop, including the fourth on a
   four-stop day — 48 of them on Red Sand. Callback stops are generated by
   `shapeMissions` and get their own reason (*"A second look at …, with what you
   have learned since"*) — 8 on Red Sand, and you neither author nor count those.

4. **Set `stakeStyle: 'brief'`** in `themes/<theme>/theme.js`. It is a hand-kept
   file; `import-book.mjs` never writes it. This does two things: it moves
   `checkStory`'s word band, and it takes the delivery line off the plan card.

5. **Import and fix the fallout.**

   ```sh
   node tools/import-book.mjs books/<theme>.yml <theme> --verify
   ```

   Expect `checkNames` to fail with a list of people whose job left the card. Fix
   each at the scene it names, re-import, repeat.

6. **Bank the reading score — if the tree is yours alone.** The pass makes
   `plainCards` better and the debt file still holds the old, looser numbers, so
   re-banking is what makes the next regression fail against the new ones:

   ```sh
   node engine/dev/plainCards.mjs --write-debt
   ```

   One caveat, and it is why this step is conditional: `--write-debt` rewrites the
   entry for **every** theme, not the one you worked on. If anybody else has cards
   in flight in the same checkout, banking freezes their half-finished numbers as
   the bar. Check `git status` first, or leave it for a clean tree.

7. **Ask whether the delivery is the right object** — see the section above. It is
   the same sitting: you are already reading the opening card, and a delivery that
   needs a gloss there is the finding.

8. **Read the fifteen cards.** The score cannot see obliqueness and it cannot see a
   sentence two that does not follow from a sentence one. `alamos-copy` is the bar.

9. **Add `delivery.meter`** — two or three words for the HUD bar, per the section
   above.

10. **Look at it.** `THEME=<theme> npm run dev`, open a day, read the plan card as a
    card and not as YAML, and check the strip reads three boxes wide.

## Traps, all of them paid for on Red Sand

- **The reading-level ratchet, not the story check, is what stops you.** Everything
  else went green on the first import; `plainCards` took three drafts. Budget for
  that.
- **A campaign's own day noun is not a time word to `checkStory`.** `sol`/`sols`
  matches nothing in its `TIME` list, so the card has to land a real cue — `since`,
  `overnight`, `before`, `three hours` — inside the first two sentences. (`watch`
  and `shift` *are* on the list, which is a trap in the other direction: a naval
  game passes this rule by accident and you learn nothing about whether the card
  actually says when it is.)
- **Deleting the cast from the card breaks `introRule` somewhere else.** Seven
  people on Red Sand. Always re-run `checkNames` after the stakes land.
- **Folded YAML breaks hyphenated words at the wrap column.** `hard-\n  won` comes
  out of the importer as `hard- won` and no check looks at it. If you wrap `reason:`
  blocks with a script, grep the book for a line ending in `-` afterwards. (One
  pre-existing instance turned up this way: a `why` block that had been printing
  *"the extra half- volt"* since the game shipped.)
- **A four-stop day needs four reasons.** Days 2, 4 and 13 of Red Sand author four,
  and the index-by-position script that writes them silently writes the wrong one if
  you assume three.
- **Cutting the stake orphans every reason that leaned on it.** This is the big one,
  and it only shows up if you read the finished card. The reasons were written while
  the long stake was still there, so they referred to *"the 40-degree request"*,
  *"the hot run"*, *"the fortnight"* — all introduced in the paragraph the pass then
  deleted. Nothing catches it: `npm run check` is green, the reading score is fine,
  and the card is quietly full of pointers to nothing. **Write the stakes first, then
  write every reason against the stake as it now stands.**
- **A self-contained line is not automatically a motivating one.** The fix for the
  dangling references was a set of plain, standalone science facts — which read
  beautifully and gave the player no reason to walk anywhere. Both failures are on
  the table above; the test for a reason is not *"can this be understood?"* but
  *"does this make me want to go?"*
- **Do not let the callback line stay generated.** Before this pass the generated
  callback reason did not exist and the card fell back to the area's `desc`, so on a
  campaign where every other call carries an authored reason the callback was the one
  line still reading as boilerplate. That is now handled in `shapeMissions`; if you
  see an area description on a plan card, the reason did not reach the stop.

## What it cost and what it bought — Red Sand

| | before | after |
| --- | --- | --- |
| stake, mean words | 125.7 | 34.7 |
| stake, mean sentences | 11.6 | 3 (a date stamp and two) |
| stake, shortest / longest | 119 / 140 words | 31 / 40 words |
| stake, mean reading grade | 4.7 | 3.5 |
| `plainCards` campaign row | mean 4.5, worst 5.8 | mean 3.4, worst 5.5 |
| cards over grade 6.5 | 0 of 16 | 0 of 16 |
| objectives whose second line is the area's job description | 48 of 48 | 0 of 48 |
| authored reasons | 0 | 48 (plus 8 callbacks, generated) |
| blocks of prose above the objectives | 2 (stake + delivery line) | 1 |
| opening card | 126 words, grade 2.1 | 68 words, grade 4.2 |
| what the campaign builds | a document about the fuel | the fuel |
| HUD readings standing over the world | 5 | 3 |

The reading score barely moves and was never the point — **the card got two thirds
shorter without getting harder**, which is the whole trade. Two book fields, one
manifest line, seven scenes given a job title, and one engine default. The syllabus
did not move.

## Where the mechanism lives

| File | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `engine/core/app.js` — `reasonFor` | picks the line under each call: authored `reason` first, then the person's role, then the group's `desc`. The book override was always there and no book used it. |
| `engine/core/app.js` — `planHTML` | assembles the card, and skips `deliveryPlanLine` when the theme is `brief`. |
| `engine/core/delivery.js` — `deliveryGainHTML` | where the delivery count still appears: the card that closes the day. |
| `tools/import-book.mjs` | carries `reason:` from the book onto the mission stop. |
| `tools/export-book.mjs` | writes it back out, so `bookParity` sees it. |
| `engine/content/normalize.js` — `shapeMissions` | decides the day's person stop, and gives the generated callback its own reason. |
| `engine/dev/checkStory.mjs` | reads `stakeStyle: 'brief'` and swaps the word band; suspends the name rule. |
| `engine/dev/plainCards.mjs` | the ratchet the pass is actually fighting. |
| `engine/dev/checkNames.mjs` | tells you exactly which scene now needs a job title. |
| `engine/dev/delivery.mjs` — `checkDelivery` | holds the delivery's shape, and requires the opening card to name it. |
| `themes/<theme>/theme.js` — `delivery` | `name`, `what`, `where`, `meter`, and one piece per day. |
| `engine/core/dashboard.js` — `updateHUD` | fills the meter from `deliveryProgress`, and keeps the old count where a theme has no delivery. |
| `index.html` — `#hudStatus` | the three boxes, and the `.hudMeter` bar. |
