# Safety Factor — play-through review

*Theme `midway` · AP Physics 1, algebra-based, all eight units (grade 12) · 15 days, ~46 stops · reviewed 2026-08-21 by reading the full book (`books/midway.yml`), solving every question and every DERIVE branch, and reading opening, stakes, warm-ups and ending in play order.*

## Verdict

The best-taught physics in the catalogue, and the clearest case in the repo for what a *place* can do to a syllabus. Seven rides, one per unit: the drop tower is kinematics, the coaster is energy, the wheel is torque, the flume is fluids. The player does not walk to a topic — they walk to a machine, and the machine happens to be the topic. Nothing else here is that clean.

Its argument is the best-balanced in the set. Marsh holds that forty-one years of safe running is evidence; Vey holds that evidence of having got away with it is not a margin. **Marsh is right on day 4** — Brennan's carousel settings turn out to be correct to within a degree of the derivation. **Vey is right on day 9** — the loop was regraded in 1998 and the drawing was never updated, so the crown demands 8.5 m/s where the 1974 paper implies 7.4, and the certificate's margin of 1.38 has actually been 1.20 for twenty-five years. Both are vindicated by arithmetic the player performs, and neither is made a fool of.

The DERIVE stops are the campaign, and they are the best-authored panels in the repo. Every wrong branch is a real student mistake that stays algebraically valid: `v = gr` (dimensionally wrong, caught by the number coming out at 73), `v = √(g/r)` (a bigger loop needing less speed), `N = mg` at the crown (a comfort criterion mistaken for a limit, 40% above the true minimum), `F = mΔvΔt` (the division inverted, giving 15.9 N where the answer is 490). The one that impressed me most survives deliberately: *"8.5 against 7.4 drawn, a difference of 1.1 m/s in the margin"* — the subtraction is correct and the framing is the defect, because quoted as a difference it reads like small change where the ratio is 15%.

**Answerable:** 46/46. Everything settles from the card.
**Sense:** Excellent within a day. Three cross-day inconsistencies about the pirate ship's drive (MW-01) and one about the wheel (MW-04).
**Level:** Right for AP Physics 1 and unusually generous with the algebra. Ten DERIVE stops is the most in the set and it is the right choice for this fiction.
**Fun:** The highest here. Certifying a shut amusement park with a hostile inspector and an owner counting advance bookings is a better frame than any disaster in the catalogue, and the props layer visibly opens the park as the campaign advances.

## The questions, solved

All arithmetic reproduces. Verified: √(2 × 9.8 × 42) = 28.7 m/s at the foot of the tower; 28.7² ÷ (2 × 9.0) = 45.8 m/s² = 4.67 g in the fins; 6 × cos 30° = 5.2 m as the torque arm; 2π × 6.2 × 8 ÷ 60 = 5.2 m/s and 5.2² ÷ 6.2 = 4.35 m/s² at the outer horse, so F = 78 × 4.35 = 340 N on the pole; 320 × 3.1 ÷ 610 = 1.63 m/s off the bumper collision; 2π√(8.6/9.8) = 5.89 s for the ship and 4π² × 8.6 ÷ 5.90² = 9.75 m/s² for g; √(2 × 9.8 × 8.6 × (1 − cos 48°)) = 7.5 m/s through the bottom of the swing and 1 + 7.5² ÷ (8.6 × 9.8) = 1.67 seat-g; √(9.8 × 7.4) = 8.52 m/s at the measured crown against √(9.8 × 5.6) = 7.41 drawn, a ratio of 1.150, and 1.38 ÷ 1.150 = 1.20 — the margin the whole campaign turns on, and it lands exactly.

Two pieces of physics I want to record as unusually good. The ship's large-amplitude correction is **right and never stated as a formula**: 5.90 s at low angle, and the 48° operating swing measured at 6.15 s, which is 5.90 × (1 + θ²/16) to three figures. The campaign refuses to let the player assume the small-angle result outside its range and makes them go and measure — that is the correct lesson and almost nothing else in the catalogue does it. And the day-12 VERIFY reconciles 4.7 g average with a 5.4 g peak by naming the mechanism: a magnetic brake makes force in proportion to speed, so it bites hardest at entry.

## Implemented since this review

- **MW-01, the ship's drive interval.** One value: 5.85 s, which makes day 12's "three tenths of a second" exact. The ending now says the drive was retimed **from 5.85 to the 6.15 the ride actually swings at**, and adds why — the small-angle formula gives 5.90 and this ride does not run at a small angle.
- **MW-02, the drop-test VERIFY.** `passRatio` 0.85 → 0.93, so the floor is 5.02 g and day 2's 4.7 g average no longer passes the stop that exists to rule it out.
- **MW-03, the EVADE card.** Retitled *Delia Marsh wants an opening date today*, with the job attached and the pronoun corrected.

And the day-9 margin CHOICE became a BALLPARK computing 10.2 ÷ 8.5 = 1.20 against 10.2 ÷ 7.4 = 1.38, so `safety factor = capacity ÷ demand` is computed. **Midway is now 12/12 equations computed with no rows in `curriculum-debt.json`**, and the conversion invariant confirms nothing was lost.
- **MW-07** numeral damage (18 occurrences, including two inside DERIVE branch notes) and **MW-08** the three place strings.

Everything else in the findings table below is unchanged, and `npm run check` is green on this theme.

## Day-by-day notes (short)

- **Day 1** — Forty-one years of notebooks and no working. The tower DERIVE gets 28.7 m/s two ways, the energy PROTOCOL reads a circuit as a ledger, and the ship CHOICE asks why sixty riders do not change a period. A model opening day: the campaign's whole thesis is stated as three questions.
- **Day 2** — What the brake has to take out. 45.8 m/s², nine metres of water, the torque arm people get wrong (the *horizontal* distance, 5.2 m, not the 6 m arm), and the HOLD.
- **Days 3–4** — Where the energy went, and Brennan vindicated. The swing-angle DERIVE arriving at an angle that depends on nothing but speed and radius — not mass, not chain length — and then agreeing with a setting made by hand in 1985, is the best single beat in the campaign.
- **Days 5–7** — The pole, the ram, the collision. The momentum DERIVE and the impulse DERIVE are correctly separated: one is what is conserved, the other is what a neck feels.
- **Day 8** — The period that ignores the load, and g measured with a boat. The cleanest use of "this local value belongs to the site, not the ride, so the tower may use it" anywhere here.
- **Day 9** — The arm and the crack, and the drive that pulls unevenly. MW-01 and MW-04 both start here.
- **Day 10** — The loop. Best day in the campaign, and the one that justifies ten DERIVE stops.
- **Days 11–13** — The impulse on a neck, the margin that moves, the wind rules, and the drop test.
- **Days 14–15** — What goes on the certificate, and what opens. The CASEBOOK sorting seven rides by what can actually be claimed is the right last question for a campaign about signatures.

## Opening and closing

Opening: "Corbin Park is an amusement park, and in October the county took away its certificate to open… The signature on all seven is yours… what you inherited is eleven notebooks of settings and timings, forty-one years of them, with not one line of working anywhere. Delia Marsh, who owns the park, says eleven million people have ridden on those numbers unhurt. Marcus Vey, who inspects for the county, calls that a record of having got away with it." Situation, authority as signature, and both sides of the argument, in one paragraph. The best opening card in the set.

Closing: three paragraphs, and the second is the model for what this document keeps asking other campaigns for — the wheel stayed shut, twenty-four gondolas stood still for a season, and Marsh borrowed against next year to cover it. The third is addressed to the player and ends "A summer of people rode safely and none of them will ever know your name. You will." Keep every word except the ship's retiming figure (MW-01).

## Warm-ups

All seven authored and all seven specific to a shut park. FOLLOW — the coaster walk-down at the pace it is actually done, where "the stops are the inspection and the walking between them is just walking" — is the best FOLLOW justification in the repo. CANVASS asks the old crew what the ship used to do at the top of the swing, which plants day 9's fault three days before it arrives. One defect, MW-03.

## What to keep

- The rides as the syllabus. Seven areas, eight units, and the player walks between them.
- Every DERIVE distractor that stays algebraically valid, and every `why` that says what number it produces.
- Marsh right on day 4 and Vey right on day 9, both settled by arithmetic.
- The ship's operating period. A formula the campaign teaches, then declines to let you use outside its range.
- 1.38 ÷ 1.150 = 1.20. A certificate that has been wrong for twenty-five years, found by taping a loop.
