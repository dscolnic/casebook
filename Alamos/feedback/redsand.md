# Red Sand — play-through review

*Theme `redsand` · AP Chemistry, the back half — thermodynamics, equilibrium, kinetics, electrochemistry (grade 12) · 15 sols, ~46 stops · reviewed 2026-08-21 by reading the full book (`books/redsand.yml`), solving every question, and reading opening, stakes, warm-ups and ending in play order.*

## Verdict

The best *stakes* in the catalogue, achieved with one sentence: everyone here goes home on a rocket that can only leave when the planets line up, it stands four hundred metres away, and it has to lift off full. Nothing else here has a deadline that cannot be argued with, and the ascent vehicle's gauge filling as the campaign advances is the single best piece of world-and-progress binding in the repo.

The chemistry is real and mostly excellent. Water splitting at 237 kJ/mol from ΔH − TΔS, Faraday's law from amps to grams of hydrogen, boil-off from a heat leak and a latent heat, freezing-point depression on a perchlorate brine, Beer–Lambert through storm dust, and the recycle-loop distinction between per-pass and overall conversion. The campaign's real subject — announced on the opening card and settled on sol 10 — is that **the thing everyone was arguing about was not the thing that was wrong**: Sundqvist wants the reactor hotter, Herrera says hotter gives back less, and the answer is that a polishing column filled around sol 261 and had been poisoning the catalyst with chloride for thirty-eight sols while everyone read the temperature profile as heat damage. That is the best-constructed misdirection in the set.

One serious problem, and it sits on the campaign's central dispute: **the equilibrium constant for the Sabatier reaction takes three mutually incompatible values across three sols, and one of them is contradicted by arithmetic the player performs themselves.** And the storm ALLOCATE — the first instrument any shipped game authored — can be passed by making no decision at all.

**Answerable:** 44/46. The two reaction-quotient stops cannot both be right (RS-01), and the ALLOCATE grades a plan nobody had to think about (RS-02).
**Sense:** Strong, with the K problem the exception. Every other thread resolves.
**Level:** Right for AP Chemistry's back half, and it computes nine of nine syllabus equations.
**Fun:** High. A finite tank, a window that does not move, and six people going home on it.

## The questions, solved

Verified: 340 L ÷ 85 L/min = 4.0 min residence; 286 − 298 × 0.163 = 237.4 kJ/mol to split water (the real value is 237.1); 13.8 × 44.0 ÷ 16.0 = 37.9 kg of CO₂ a sol; ΔG = −165,000 − 593 × (−172) = −63.0 kJ/mol at the bed's temperature (and the reaction stops wanting to run at 165,000 ÷ 172 = 959 K, which is the honest answer to that stop's title); ΔT_f = 3 × 1.86 × 8.0 = 44.6 K on the brine; 30 W × 88,800 s ÷ 213,000 J/kg = 12.5 kg of oxygen a sol out of the tank (and 88,800 s is a sol to three figures, which I appreciated); 640 A × 88,800 s ÷ 192,970 C/mol × 2.016 g/mol = 594 g of hydrogen a cell a sol; 6.9 kg × 55 kWh/kg = 380 kWh; 490 × e^(−2.4) = 44 W/m² of direct beam through storm dust; 2 × 32.0 ÷ 16.0 = 4.0 kg of oxygen per kg of methane.

The sol-9 CONTROL is the best-posed stop here: change the residence time at fixed temperature and pressure, and see whether conversion follows it back on reversal — which is exactly the experiment that separates a kinetic limit from an equilibrium one, and it is the experiment Herrera's argument needs and never got.

## Implemented since this review

- **RS-01, the equilibrium constant.** One enthalpy for the whole campaign: ΔH = −123.6 kJ/mol against ΔS = −172 gives K = 1,350 at 533 K, 192 at 573 and 80 at 593 — which is what sols 3 and 6 already claimed to their own rounding, and what the sol-5 ΔG now computes (−21.6 kJ/mol, crossing zero at 720 K). Q = 282 is still above the hot ceiling and below the cool one, so Herrera's argument is unchanged and now self-consistent.
- **RS-02, the storm ALLOCATE.** Pool 430 → 600 kWh and the drier made a second required answer. Habitat 180 (protected) + liquefier 210 + drier 40 = 430 forced, leaving 170 — which funds the stacks at half rate (160), or the battery reserve with the downlink (140), or the drill with the downlink (110). Three plans, one substantial job each, and the grade now turns on a decision.
- **RS-04**, the Q target: 283 → 282.24, the exact product.

Everything else in the findings table below is unchanged, and `npm run check` is green on this theme.

## Sol-by-sol notes (short)

- **Sols 1–2** — 2,700 kilograms short. Rate against yield stated on the first sol, which is the whole argument, and the drier-and-CO₂-trap CHOICE that explains why the cold end exists before anything freezes in it.
- **Sol 3** — The line that stopped passing gas. The DIAGNOSIS (water past the drier freezing in the exchanger) is well built, and RS-01 starts here.
- **Sols 4–5** — Feed rate, the charge ledger's missing 8%, and the ceiling on running hotter. The catalyst CHOICE — *equilibrium arrives sooner and the mixture settles at the same place* — is the cleanest statement of what a catalyst cannot do anywhere here.
- **Sol 6** — Water is the whole plant. Brine, overpotential as heat, and the Q-versus-K direction call (RS-01).
- **Sols 7–8** — Boil-off, RWGS selectivity, and reading a bed by where its heat is. The exchange-front CHOICE (the outlet rises toward the inlet concentration as the front reaches the end) is the mechanism sol 10 needs.
- **Sols 9–10** — The assay bench and the trace nobody read. **The best two sols in the campaign.** The chloride trace's shape says the column filled around sol 261 and has passed feed straight through since, and the CONTROL then separates kinetics from equilibrium properly. This is the payoff and it lands.
- **Sols 11–12** — A new charge, a rule for the water, and the storm. RS-02 and RS-03 sit here.
- **Sols 13–15** — The radiator, loading, and what the next crew is told. The TRIGGER — write the rule before the trace moves — is the right last instrument for a campaign whose whole failure was a trace nobody read.

## Opening and closing

Opening: "Everyone here goes home on a rocket that can only leave when Mars and Earth line up. The next alignment is sixteen months off. It stands on the pad four hundred metres away and has to lift off full. Nothing is flown from Earth to fill it… So far the plant has made 3.9." Deadline, mechanism, authority, and both sides of the argument. This is the rewritten card and it is now one of the two or three best in the set.

Closing: three paragraphs and all three do work. The middle one names the cost honestly — eleven sols of production, one catalyst charge with no replacement on the planet, four hundred kilograms of oxygen out of the vent — and the unfinished list is the best in the repo: a change interval that is a guess taken from one breakthrough, an alarm wired four sols before the rotation ended that has never sounded, and nobody knowing what the deeper ice does to a catalyst. The last paragraph is addressed to the player. Keep all of it.
