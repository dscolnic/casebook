PROJECT Y (theme: projecty) — PLACES AND LOCATIONS
Los Alamos · 1943–45 · five divisions
Course: not a syllabus — the wartime laboratory's own subjects, split five ways:
neutron physics and uncertainty, counting statistics and diagnostics, isotopes
and materials handling, interfaces and qualification testing, and the systems
integration that has to survive all four being put together.
Grade 12. One mission is called a Stage. Fifteen of them are programme stages
rather than days.
Delivery: The Evidence Chain — the board is in T. 15 pieces, one a stage.

Run it:  cd gamekit && THEME=projecty npm run dev

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS PLACE IS THIS PLACE  (from the source file that builds it)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
site.js — Los Alamos, as data.

`engine/world/outdoorTown.js` builds the mesa, the roads, the pond, the
nineteen buildings and the sky from what is in site.js. Everything that is
*this place* rather than the shape of the ground — the Tech Area wire and its
guard towers, the water tank, the catenary power lines, coal bins, laundry
lines, duckboard walks, jeeps, bicycles, the Ponderosa forest, the road
lighting — is code in props.js beside it. **This site is the one that is not
fully declarative**: the forest, the ground scatter and the lamp positions are
JavaScript, not site data.

Positions and dimensions were read out of the running game before the flip —
`buildingMeshes` for position and angle, `BUILDING_DATA` for size and colour —
so this reproduces the town rather than reinterpreting it. The engine's `mesa`
profile was checked against the heightfield `env.js` computed by hand over 841
points first: mean difference 0.06 m, worst case 0.5 m, and the only half-metre
cases were building pads where the old surface noise dipped a bench that should
read level.

**The geography is the secrecy.** Three of the five divisions — T, CM and E —
stand inside one wired band across the north of town, entered through a badge
gate on the road. P and X sat in their own smaller wired compounds east and
west, as several outlying sites did. Everything between them is the town the
Army had to build to keep its staff on the mesa: dorms, hutments, trailers, a
chapel, an infirmary, a theatre, a post exchange. Walking from a division to a
division is walking through the housing of the people who worked in it.

are ahead, the Tech Area is behind:

Load-bearing rather than decorative:

  · The spawn at (0, 14) is on Trinity Drive with the road running through it.
    The bicycle rack outside Fuller Lodge is placed through `clearSpot` with an
    explicit 12 m avoid circle on the spawn for this reason. A prop over the
    spawn welds the player in place: the scene renders perfectly and W does
    nothing.
  · Every `group` below is a group id in content/groups.js. All five have a
    building, which is what makes every call reachable; a group with no
    building is a call the player cannot reach, and `worldParity` is the only
    check that notices.
  · **`ROADS`, `onRoad` and `MESA_PLAYER_LIMIT` in props.js look dead from
    outside and are not.** `plantTrees` reads all three to decide where a tree
    may stand, which is why the roads are declared twice — once as
    `site.paths` for the engine to grade, once there as the rectangles the
    forest keeps out of.
  · The horizon is two ranges and they are nothing alike: the Jemez rise
    immediately west, close and dark, and the Sangre de Cristo stand forty
    miles east across the Rio Grande valley, high and blued out. The skyline is
    the whole of wayfinding on a flat mesa.
  · `look.far` is 2600. This site scales its sky dome to 850, so at an
    interior's 160 — or even at 900 — the dome is clipped and the sky renders
    **black in broad daylight**, with no error anywhere.

walk into.

THE DELIVERY — The Evidence Chain
==============================================================================
What the laboratory has to be able to show at the end: every claim from a
counted signal to a frozen design, and how well each one of them is known.
One link a stage, in the order the work happened to establish them — each
piece carries how well it is known rather than what it concluded.

==============================================================================
AUTHORING NOTES
==============================================================================
· A lesson points at a fixture with `at: <id>` in its book. This theme has no
  fixtures.js, so no lesson can point at one and all 52 stops are answered
  at their room's case stand. Adding fixtures here is a `fixtures.js` plus
  `at:` on the lessons; `placement.mjs` is the gate.
· Every `group` here must exist in content/groups.js or its calls are
  unreachable; worldParity and reachable are what notice. All five do.
· Never place a prop over the spawn or across the main walking line. Trinity
  Drive runs through the spawn, so anything placed near (0, 14) has to go
  through `clearSpot` with the spawn in its `avoid` list, as the bicycle rack
  does.
· `kit.js` placers take `(x, z, y)` — ground last.
· Anything added to the sky here must be unlit. A lit object under this site's
  bright dome renders black with a highlight on it.
· The roster is real historical people — 26 bios, every one of them carrying a
  `quiz`, plus 5 leaders. Nothing may be put in one of their mouths that the
  record does not support. Note that this theme sets no `theme.history` and no
  `real: true` on the roster, which the discovery games do; the rule holds
  anyway, and nothing enforces it here.
