ReadingWhere this campaign stands

Both slates are applied, and the rule is a check now. Before them, day 1's first stop asked what a falling frequency trend is evidence of and the card named c20 · inertia, governor response and droop control (twice) and c31 · metering — layer 5 of 6 — while c19 · frequency as the balance of supply and demand, which droop is built out of, was claimed by no stop until day 12, and c18 · synchronous machines by no stop at all. Today that stop claims the balance itself, which is what it computes, and says on the card that it takes synchronous machines and power-against-energy as read. engine/dev/conceptOrder.mjs is green on all 29 themes.

The numbers: of 28 prerequisite inversions, 12 rested on material this campaign itself teaches later and 16 on the bottom of the graph. The first group is ordered — 0 left — and the second is declared, in 32 takesAsRead entries that the importer refuses unless the concept is genuinely something the stop's claim is built out of. Concepts claimed went 22 → 25 of 32; ρ went 0.19 → 0.29.

The gate is per stop, and that is not the same audit as this document's. Two stops can claim one concept, and each of them has to answer for its own prerequisites — so conceptOrder found 6 rows the concept-level count had hidden, including a stop standing beside its own base on day 6 rather than after it. Four were declared; two were ordered, and one of those is the fix worth copying: day 10's dark-hours estimate now claims energy from power over time, which is what E = Pt is, and that single re-claim put the base under two later stops at once.

The finding that decides the work

Re-ordering the story is not the fix. The fortnight is a chronology — the trip causes the aftermath, the island precedes the black start, the report is last — and searching permutations of the fifteen missions that respect those 11 constraints, the best correlation available is ρ = 0.46 against today's 0.29 — and measured before the pass it left 10 of the 12 in-course inversions standing, which is why it was never the fix. The re-order that was taken scores 0.29 rather than that ceiling on purpose: the search ranked orders by prerequisite breaks first and correlation second, and the correlation-maximal order teaches backwards. Ignore the story entirely and it reaches 0.50; move individual stops rather than whole missions and it reaches 0.90, at 30 rewritten scenes.

Correlation is the wrong target anyway — it is the cheap number adjacent to the thing. The target is the prerequisite: every concept a stop claims has a base that arrived on an earlier day, or the stop says out loud that it assumes it. That is what the two slates did, without moving a mission or touching the story: 12 in-course inversions down to 0 — two swapped pairs, one rewritten question, and the last row declared rather than moved, because day 1 cannot have anything before it.

Gateengine/dev/conceptOrder.mjs

One rule, and it is the rule equationOrder already enforces one field over: for every stop that claims concept c on day d, each of c's prerequisites is claimed by a stop on a day earlier than d, or is named in that stop's own assumes. The hatch is what makes the rule satisfiable, and it is the right hatch — assumes already exists, is already printed, and turns "the player has not met this" into an authored sentence. Both halves get counted: the failures, and the exceptions, because a campaign with sixty exceptions has not passed anything.

Two reports beside it and neither of them a gate: the rank correlation, and the layer band. The selftest is the load-bearing half — three cases, each of them a bug this file could have. A fixture whose prerequisite is claimed on the same day must fail, because the engine opens a day in any order. One claimed a day earlier must pass. One declared in assumes must pass, with the exception counted and printed. Put each bug back and that case, and only that case, should fail.

Debt rather than an advisory flag: 0 rows on one game is too many to gate on cold, so what exists today goes in concept-debt.json beside curriculum-debt.json, with the same two properties — a row not on the list fails immediately, and a row on the list that has since been fixed also fails, naming the line to delete. It only shrinks.

The order of work

1. Author the claim: concept: on a stop in the book, refused by import-book.mjs when it names something not on the theme's syllabus rather than dropped, with pickKeyConcept as the fallback. Nothing here can be built before this — a gate on a derived field grades the matcher, not the course. 2. Review the 32 needs lists into tools/syllabus.js; the graph in this document is one reading of one syllabus. 3. conceptOrder.mjs with its three selftest cases, and concept-debt.json baselined at today's 0. 4. node engine/dev/curriculumDelivery.mjs blackout --snapshot before.json. 5. Slates A then B, editing the book and re-importing — node tools/import-book.mjs books/blackout.yml blackout --verify. The two written questions are a deliberate curriculum change: re-snapshot after them and say so in the commit. 6. --against before.json, knowing what it will and will not say — a corrected claim is invisible to it, a reworded takeaway fails it, and a changed day is not compared at all, so add the day comparison to diffSnapshots as a note before slate C, never as a failure. 7. npm run check blackout, then npm run drive blackout on the two new questions, right answer and wrong. 8. Re-derive the editions. blackout_fable shares this syllabus and has the same day-1 opening; blackout_ms claims no concepts at all, because none of its 16 concepts has a t written — its cards show no key-concept door, and this whole audit is unmeasurable there until they do.
