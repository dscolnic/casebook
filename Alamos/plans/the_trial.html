ReadingWhere this campaign stands

Twenty-four CHOICE stops out of 45 and 5 of 11 equations computed. Three gaps sit on instruments and may clear in Phase 0; three do not, and they are the three that decide whether a trial can answer its question — alpha spending, the events formula, and conditional power. The place is the hardest constraint in the set: one long floor, no vehicles, and the walk down it is distance from the patient. That rules out the driven formats and leaves the ones that run on data, which is fortunate, because this is AP Statistics.

FunWhat this subject and this place can carry

SWEEP

strongstop 29 or 34 · STAT

Drag the sample size and watch the interval narrow — and it narrows as the square root, which is the one thing about precision every student gets wrong. "How big, and how wide" is currently a BALLPARK; the curve is the answer and it is plotted only where you look.

serves
SE = √(p(1−p)/n) and CI ≈ estimate ± 1.96 × SE — two gaps in one panel
needs
the diminishing return visible in the shape, not stated in the hint

TALLY

strongstop 16 · STAT

Events accumulate and the analysis becomes possible. "How much of the trial has happened" is a BALLPARK about exactly this, and TALLY’s own subject — when is there enough to report — is interim analysis.

serves
the events formula and alpha spending
needs
an early look that flatters the result

SPOT

strongstop 10 or 40 · STAT / SAFE

A stopping rule replaced mid-trial with nothing announcing it. This is the sharpest statistical version of the format anywhere: an unannounced change to a stopping rule is how alpha gets spent without anybody deciding to spend it.

serves
Σ α_spent ≤ α — a budget for error that does not refill
risk
score only the discriminating items

HOLDOUT

strongstop 34 · STAT

"The subgroup that crossed" is a CHOICE, and subgroup analysis is the canonical overfitting story in clinical research. Fit the subgroup rule on one half, freeze it, score it on the half it never saw, and the flattering answer costs the stop.

serves
multiplicity and subgroup discipline
needs
a spike in the fitting curve that beats the honest answer

BELT

mediumSITE · monitoring

Case report forms sorted against one criterion — protocol deviation or not — at the speed a monitoring visit runs. Real trial work, and one honest bit. Medium because the criterion in practice needs more than one clause.

risk
a definition needing two clauses is not a BELT

The order of work

1. Phase 0 — teach the coverage check to read an instrument’s own board, and re-baseline curriculum-debt.json. Rewriting a stop that already teaches the thing is the exact failure the check exists to prevent. 2. Snapshot. 3. Edit the book, never the generated content — tools/import-book.mjs books/the_trial.yml the_trial --verify. 4. npm run traps: every instrument’s trap must fire. 5. npm run check the_trial and npm run drive the_trial, right answer and wrong — ask what a player who understood nothing would score before believing any pass mark. 6. --against the snapshot. A changed format is reported; a changed objective fails.
