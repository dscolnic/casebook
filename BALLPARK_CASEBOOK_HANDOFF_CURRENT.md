## 1. Goal

For every block in `ballpark_casebook_manifest.md`, create **one Ballpark topic game on the same domain**.

Each Casebook case becomes one object in the engine’s `GAMES` array:

- Casebook case: `e_quake`
- Ballpark game id: `bp_e_quake`

The Casebook and Ballpark should feel related, but Ballpark must not reveal the Casebook solution, culprit, concealed cause, or clue answers.

The manifest remains the subject-matter source. Use its:

- discipline;
- scenario;
- science taught;
- suggested estimand;
- proposed handles.

However, the manifest’s old “three handles for one estimand” structure is only a source of ideas. The current game requires **four related but independent estimation questions**, not three methods converging on one answer.

## 2. Current Ballpark mechanic

Each topic game contains:

- **four related estimation questions**;
- **exactly two 2-factor equations**;
- **exactly two 3-factor equations**;
- **10 factor slots total**;
- a shared, shuffled bank of **10 numerical choices**;
- fixed factor labels and fixed operators already visible;
- three rounds of play;
- final-answer feedback: **Right**, **Too high**, **Too low**, or **Right total, misplaced**;
- component-level feedback that identifies and locks each correctly placed value;
- free educational context and vocabulary;
- a final reveal with the correct equations and explanations;
- a sticky number bank on the right on desktop and an accessible responsive layout on smaller screens.

The player is not inventing factors, choosing operators, or moving sliders. The player’s task is to match each numerical value to the correct named component.

## 5. Topic structure

Every game must contain exactly four questions on one coherent topic.

A strong four-question set usually covers four different dimensions, such as:

1. **scale or total** — volume, population, mass, energy, count;
2. **rate or throughput** — flow, production, requests, treatments, vehicles per hour;
3. **time, range, or endurance** — travel time, operating duration, battery life;
4. **resource, consequence, or secondary quantity** — material, data, cost, force, emissions, staffing.

The four questions must not merely calculate the same master quantity four different ways.

### Good topic example: flood science

- total water passing the town;
- flood-wave travel time;
- runoff generated by the catchment;
- sediment transported.

### Weak topic example

- flood volume from discharge;
- flood volume from reservoir drawdown;
- flood volume from rainfall;
- flood volume from inundation.

That older pattern belonged to the convergence game. It is repetitive in the current format.

---

## 6. Difficulty target

The game should be approachable for a curious high-school student or introductory college student.

Required composition:

- two equations with **2 factors**;
- two equations with **3 factors**;
- no equation with 1 factor;
- no equation with 4 or more factors;
- exactly 10 values in the shared bank.

The challenge should come from understanding the factors, units, and scale—not from obscure terminology, tedious conversions, or deliberately deceptive arithmetic.

Use ordinary multiplication and division. Addition is technically supported by the engine but should be rare and used only when it represents a genuinely meaningful sum. Prefer `×` and `÷`.

## 8. Field definitions

### Topic fields

- `id`: exactly the manifest id, beginning with `bp_`.
- `title`: concise player-facing topic title.
- `tag`: three or four short domain handles separated by `·`.
- `context`: spoiler-free educational framing. Do not include bank values or answers.
- `terms`: usually 4–6 `[term, plain-language definition]` pairs.
- `eqs`: exactly four equation objects.

### Equation fields

- `id`: unique within the topic; lowercase snake_case is preferred.
- `q`: player-facing estimation question with no numerical givens.
- `unit`: final displayed answer unit.
- `factors`: exactly two or three factor objects.
- `ops`: one fewer operator than factors.
- `answer`: exact numeric result produced by the stored factor values and operators.
- `answerDisplay`: readable formatted result.
- `explain`: one or two sentences explaining the dimensional logic and scientific meaning.

### Factor fields

- `id`: globally unique within the topic; use `<equation_id>_f<index>`.
- `label`: concise name shown above the slot.
- `unit`: human-readable unit or dimensionless descriptor.
- `value`: numeric JavaScript value used for arithmetic and correctness.
- `display`: exact text shown in the number bank.
- `desc`: plain-language explanation of what the factor represents and why it belongs.

`value` controls correctness. `display` controls presentation. They must represent the same number.

---

## 9. No numerical leakage

Before the reveal, no visible prose may disclose a bank value or final answer.

Check all of these:

- title;
- tag;
- context;
- vocabulary definitions;
- question wording;
- factor labels;
- factor descriptions;
- explanatory instructions.

### Bad

> What drainage rate removes 80% of the water in 30 minutes?

If `0.8` and `30` are bank values, the question has already placed them conceptually.

### Better

> How quickly must the emergency drainage system remove the accumulated runoff?

The factor labels can be:

- runoff volume;
- removable fraction;
- operating time.

But the visible prose must not disclose their values.

### Allowed numbers

Numbers are allowed when they are not bank clues, such as:

- a proper name like `Apollo 11`;
- unit notation like `m²`;
- a Casebook title containing a number;
- the interface phrase `Round 1 of 3`.

Still avoid avoidable numeric wording when it may confuse the player.

---

## 10. No answer chaining

A result from one equation may never be an input to another equation in the same game.

This prohibition includes:

- the exact result;
- a rounded version of the result;
- a converted version of the result;
- the result renamed as another factor;
- a factor that secretly bundles the previous answer.

### Bad

1. Calculate annual solar energy.
2. Use “annual solar energy” to calculate homes served.

### Better

1. Estimate annual solar energy from rated power, capacity factor, and hours.
2. Estimate homes served independently from panel count, annual delivered yield per panel, and household demand.

The second equation may address the same system, but its inputs must stand on their own.

---

## 11. Independent questions and independent inputs

Every factor must be a meaningful observation, estimate, physical quantity, conversion, or modeling assumption.

Do not use:

- “the total from Question 1”;
- “combined amount calculated above”;
- a manufactured rate equal to two other tiles multiplied together;
- a factor that exists only to disguise answer chaining;
- a precise fictional quantity presented as a measured historical fact.

The four questions should teach different concepts from the manifest’s `Science taught` list.

The manifest’s suggested estimand will often make a good Question 1. Use the other science concepts to design Questions 2–4.

---

## 12. Meaningful 2- and 3-factor equations

Every factor must earn its place.

Good 2-factor shapes:

- count × amount per item;
- rate × time;
- total ÷ amount per item;
- distance ÷ speed;
- area × loading per area;
- power × time.

Good 3-factor shapes:

- count × rate per item × time;
- area × depth × fraction;
- power × efficiency × time;
- mass × energy per mass × conversion;
- population × participation fraction × frequency;
- rate × duration ÷ conversion.

Avoid:

- multiplying by 1;
- a gratuitous conversion that could be absorbed without educational value;
- two conversion factors in one equation;
- arbitrary “adjustment multipliers” whose meaning is vague;
- factors created only to reach three components;
- trivial percentages directly stated in the question.

At most one routine conversion factor should appear in an equation unless unit conversion is itself the lesson.

---

## 13. Number-bank quality

The 10 factor values become one shared number bank.

Required:

- exactly 10 factors total;
- each factor id is unique;
- each numeric `value` is unique within the topic;
- each `display` is unique within the topic;
- each value is used exactly once;
- no final `answer` equals any factor `value`;
- values are distinguishable enough to manipulate;
- values use an appropriate mix of scales without making placement automatic.

### Honest uniqueness

Never alter a real conversion or scientific constant merely to avoid duplication.

If two equations naturally need the same value, redesign one equation. Do not replace `1,000` with `999` or `1,001`.

### Avoid duplicate-looking values

Even numerically distinct values such as `5`, `5.0`, and `5.00` are unacceptable. They look identical in play.

Avoid banks dominated by obvious pairs such as:

- `24`, `48`, `72`, `96`;
- `1,000`, `10,000`, `100,000`;
- several percentages clustered near one another.

Some similarity is useful, but the bank should remain fair.

## 14. Vocabulary and educational help

The educational layer is free and should reduce background-knowledge barriers without solving the game.

Define terms a high-school student may not know.

For each factor, explain:

1. what it represents;
2. why it belongs in the equation;
3. what its unit means.

Do not define a factor by giving its magnitude.

### Good

**Discharge**  
The volume of water passing a point during a unit of time.

### Bad

**Discharge**  
About 480 cubic metres per second during this flood.

Prefer plain language before jargon. Technical terms can remain when the definition makes them accessible.

## 16. Accuracy, assumptions, and sourcing

Use defensible order-of-magnitude values.

Because many Casebook settings are fictional, it is acceptable to create a coherent synthetic model. In that case:

- use rounded values;
- describe them as representative, modeled, or planning-scale estimates;
- keep physical relationships realistic;
- avoid false precision;
- do not claim the fictional value came from a real measurement.

For real constants, conversions, or typical ranges:

- use trustworthy references;
- preserve the true conversion;
- retain source notes during authoring;
- prefer a broad, honest approximation to an unsupported precise number.

Optional non-rendered metadata may be added at topic level:

```js
sourceNotes: [
  "Typical flood-wave speeds based on broad open-channel order-of-magnitude ranges.",
  "All Rossmere quantities are fictional rounded case-model assumptions."
]
```

The current engine ignores extra metadata, so it will not affect gameplay.

---

## 17. Arithmetic and operator rules

The engine evaluates operations from left to right.

For factors `[A, B, C]` and:

```js
ops: ["×", "÷"]
```

the result is:

```text
(A × B) ÷ C
```

Do not assume conventional algebraic precedence beyond the written left-to-right chain.

Required:

```js
ops.length === factors.length - 1
```

Allowed operators:

- `"×"`
- `"÷"`
- `"+"` only when genuinely appropriate

Do not divide by zero. Avoid negative quantities unless the domain specifically requires them and the player-facing meaning is clear.

Store `answer` using the exact numeric values. Format only `answerDisplay`.

---

## 18. Feedback integrity

Correctness is semantic, not merely arithmetic.

A question is fully correct only when every value is in the correct named factor slot.

If swapped values accidentally produce the correct final total, the engine reports:

> Right total, misplaced

Correct individual slots lock after each round. Therefore:

- do not reuse the same numeric value in two slots;
- do not create factors whose labels are semantically interchangeable;
- do not write vague labels such as `Amount A` and `Amount B`;
- make every factor meaning distinct.

The final-answer high/low feedback is useful only when equations behave monotonically and sensibly. Avoid fragile chains in which swapping values creates extreme, undefined, or nonsensical results.

## 20. Suggested automated validator

Use a validator equivalent to the following before publishing:

```js
function calcEquation(e) {
  let result = e.factors[0].value;
  e.ops.forEach((op, i) => {
    const next = e.factors[i + 1].value;
    if (op === "×") result *= next;
    else if (op === "÷") result /= next;
    else if (op === "+") result += next;
    else throw new Error(`Unsupported operator: ${op}`);
  });
  return result;
}

function nearlyEqual(a, b) {
  return Math.abs(a - b) <= Math.max(1e-9, Math.abs(b) * 1e-9);
}

function validateGame(g) {
  const errors = [];
  const eqs = g.eqs || [];
  const factors = eqs.flatMap(e => e.factors || []);
  const values = factors.map(f => f.value);
  const displays = factors.map(f => f.display);
  const ids = [g.id, ...eqs.map(e => e.id), ...factors.map(f => f.id)];

  if (eqs.length !== 4) errors.push("must have exactly four equations");

  const lengths = eqs.map(e => e.factors.length).sort();
  if (JSON.stringify(lengths) !== JSON.stringify([2, 2, 3, 3])) {
    errors.push("factor counts must be exactly [2,2,3,3]");
  }

  if (factors.length !== 10) errors.push("must have exactly ten factors");
  if (new Set(values).size !== 10) errors.push("numeric values must be unique");
  if (new Set(displays).size !== 10) errors.push("display strings must be unique");
  if (new Set(ids).size !== ids.length) errors.push("ids must be unique");

  eqs.forEach(e => {
    if (e.ops.length !== e.factors.length - 1) {
      errors.push(`${e.id}: wrong operator count`);
    }

    const calculated = calcEquation(e);
    if (!Number.isFinite(calculated)) {
      errors.push(`${e.id}: result is not finite`);
    } else if (!nearlyEqual(calculated, e.answer)) {
      errors.push(`${e.id}: stored answer does not match arithmetic`);
    }
  });

  const answers = eqs.map(e => e.answer);
  answers.forEach((answer, i) => {
    if (values.some(v => nearlyEqual(v, answer))) {
      errors.push(`${eqs[i].id}: answer is reused as a factor value`);
    }
  });

  return {
    ok: errors.length === 0,
    errors,
    equationCount: eqs.length,
    factorPattern: lengths,
    bankSize: factors.length
  };
}
```

Automated validation cannot detect all semantic problems. A human or language-model audit must still check leakage, answer chaining, factor meaning, plausibility, topic coherence, and spoilers.

## 21. Manifest conversion recipe

For each manifest case:

### Step 1 — Read the whole block

Extract:

- id;
- title;
- discipline;
- scenario;
- real cause and bait, for internal spoiler control;
- science taught;
- suggested estimand;
- three proposed handles.

### Step 2 — Choose four estimands

Use the suggested estimand as one candidate.

Choose three more from the science-taught concepts. Prefer different dimensions of the system.

Example for an earthquake case:

- released energy;
- rupture length or area;
- seismic-wave travel time;
- number or spacing of monitoring stations.

Do not make all four questions different ways to estimate earthquake energy.

### Step 3 — Draft equations before choosing numbers

For each question, write a meaningful 2- or 3-factor chain with units.

Ensure the final pattern across the topic is exactly:

```text
2 factors
2 factors
3 factors
3 factors
```

The order can vary.

### Step 4 — Select honest values

Choose plausible, rounded values.

Check that:

- every value is unique;
- the result is meaningful;
- no result is reused;
- the bank is fair;
- the question does not disclose the values.

### Step 5 — Write educational support

Add:

- concise context;
- 4–6 vocabulary terms;
- clear factor descriptions;
- a dimensional explanation for each equation.

### Step 6 — Remove spoilers and leaks

Search the complete object for:

- all 10 display values;
- all four answers;
- the Casebook real cause;
- bait and conclusion language.

### Step 7 — Validate and test

Run the structural and arithmetic validator, then play all three rounds in the browser.
